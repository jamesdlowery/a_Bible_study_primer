# 2 Chronicles: Textual and Translation Differences

---

## 2 Chronicles 22:2 — was Ahaziah twenty-two, or forty-two, when he became king?

**The variant:** MT's Chronicles text gives Ahaziah's age as "forty-two." The parallel, textually stable account in 2 Kings 8:26 gives "twenty-two." A number of Hebrew manuscripts, the Septuagint, and the Syriac all read "twenty-two" at this verse in Chronicles as well. The "forty-two" reading additionally creates an internal contradiction within Chronicles itself: two verses earlier (21:20), Ahaziah's own father is said to have died at age 40 — making MT's Chronicles text have the son older than the father. Most scholars regard "forty-two" as a straightforward transmission error.

**How the translations render it:**
- **"Forty-two" (retaining MT's Chronicles reading as written):** KJV, NKJV. **Douay-Rheims, the RSV, and Darby** are also in this camp, along with **Smith's Literal Translation** ("The son of forty and two years was Ahaziah in his reigning"). **CPDV** is also in this camp ("Ahaziah was forty-two years old when he had begun to reign"). **AKJV** is also in this camp ("Forty and two years old was Ahaziah when he began to reign"), matching KJV's wording exactly, as expected given its preservation method. **WEB** is also in this camp ("Forty-two years old was Ahaziah when he began to reign") -- a genuinely notable result given WEB is a modern (1997) translation that here retains the harder Masoretic reading rather than joining the modern "twenty-two" harmonization most other contemporary translations adopt. **Webster's Bible** is also in this camp ("Forty and two years old was Ahaziah when he began to reign"), matching KJV's wording exactly. **CJB** is also in this camp: "Achazyah was forty-two years old when he began his reign." **The NRSV-CE** is also in this camp, checked against its own text: "Ahaziah was forty-two years old when he began to reign; he reigned one year in Jerusalem."
- **"Twenty-two" (adopting the Septuagint/Syriac reading, harmonizing with 2 Kings 8:26):** ESV, NIV, NASB, CSB, NLT — generally with a footnote noting the Hebrew reads "forty-two." **ASV and the BSB** are also in this camp, along with **the ERV** ("He was 22 years old when he began to rule," with its own footnote marker acknowledging the textual issue) and **the NET Bible** ("Ahaziah was twenty-two years old when he became king"). **Young's Literal Translation** is also in this camp ("A son of twenty and two years") -- a genuinely notable result given YLT's own general reputation for extreme literalism and preference for the harder Masoretic reading elsewhere in this book, yet here it joins the harmonized reading instead. **The NRSV-CE does not join this camp**: like its NRSV parent, and unlike most modern critical-text translations, it retains the Masoretic "forty-two" (see the "forty-two" camp above). **AMP** is also in this camp: "Ahaziah was [fn]twenty-two years old when he became king," with a footnote reading "According to some versions and 2 Kin 8:26; Heb forty-two years, but this is most likely a copyist's error." **LSB** is also in this camp: "Ahaziah was [fn]twenty-two years old when he became king," with the same footnote convention as AMP. **NABRE** is also in this same camp: "Ahaziah was twenty-two years old when he became king."
- **RSV2CE** is also in the "forty-two" camp (confirmed in the RSV Catholic Edition text that RSV2CE carries forward; the Second Catholic Edition's 2006 revision was confined to archaic pronouns and verb forms and to certain liturgical passages, none of which touches this verse): "Ahaziah was forty-two years old when he began to reign."

**The difference in meaning:** With "forty-two," the passage contains an internal chronological impossibility (a son older than his father). With "twenty-two," the passage is chronologically coherent and matches the parallel account in Kings.

## 2 Chronicles 36:9 — was Jehoiachin eight, or eighteen, when he became king?

**The variant:** MT's Chronicles text gives Jehoiachin's age as "eight." The parallel account in 2 Kings 24:8 gives "eighteen." A number of Hebrew manuscripts, plus Greek and Syriac witnesses, read "eighteen" at this verse in Chronicles too. The likely mechanical cause: the Hebrew word for "-teen" could easily drop out in copying, especially since a later corrective marginal note may have been misapplied instead to the number of days in his reign — which is why Chronicles specifies "three months and ten days" where Kings simply says "three months."

**How the translations render it:**
- **"Eight" (retaining MT's Chronicles reading as written):** KJV, NKJV. **Darby and Douay-Rheims** are also in this camp, along with **YLT** ("a son of eight years," explicitly noted by at least one commentator as the volume's own characteristically literal rendering) and **Smith's Literal Translation** ("The son of eight years was Jehoiachin in his reigning"). **CPDV** is also in this camp ("Jehoiachin was eight years old when he had begun to reign"). **AKJV** is also in this camp ("Jehoiachin was eight years old"), matching KJV's wording exactly, as expected given its preservation method. **WEB** is also in this camp ("Jehoiachin was eight years old when he began to reign"), matching the AKJV's wording almost exactly. **Webster's Bible** is also in this camp ("Jehoiachin was eight years old when he began to reign"), matching KJV's wording exactly. **NRSV-CE** is also in this same camp: "Jehoiachin was eight years old when he began to reign" -- a genuinely notable divergence from its own RSV cousin, since (as confirmed below) the RSV itself reads "eighteen" at this same verse, meaning NRSV-CE and RSV/RSV2CE land in opposite camps here despite their shared translation lineage. **CJB** is also in this same camp: "Y'hoyakhin was eight years old when he began his reign." **The RSV itself** is also in this camp, checked against its own text ("Jehoiachin was eight years old when he began to reign"), consistent with its retention of the Masoretic "forty-two" at the Ahaziah verse above; and **RSV2CE** reads the same (confirmed in the RSV Catholic Edition text that RSV2CE carries forward; the Second Catholic Edition's 2006 revision was confined to archaic pronouns and verb forms and to certain liturgical passages, none of which touches this verse).
- **"Eighteen" (adopting the Greek/Syriac/Hebrew-manuscript alternate, harmonizing with 2 Kings 24:8):** ESV, NASB, CSB, NIV, NLT. **ASV and the BSB** are also in this camp, along with **the NABRE**. (The footnote "Septuagint (compare 2 Kings 24:8); most Hebrew manuscripts eight years old" belongs to the ESV, not the RSV: the RSV itself reads "eight" here -- see the "eight" camp above.) **ERV** is also in this "eighteen" camp ("Jehoiachin was 18 years old when he became king of Judah"). **NET Bible** is also in this camp ("Jehoiachin was eighteen years old when he became king"). **AMP** straddles both camps with its own distinctive bracketed presentation, printing both readings in one word: "Jehoiachin was eight[teen] years old when he became king."
- **LSB** is in the "eight" camp: "Jehoiachin was eight years old when he became king, and he reigned three months and ten days in Jerusalem," with no footnote at this verse in its own published text. **AMP** occupies a position of its own: its main text reads "Jehoiachin was eight[teen] years old when he became king," using its bracket-amplification convention to print the Masoretic "eight" and the harmonized "eighteen" together in a single word rather than committing to either.

**The difference in meaning:** An 8-year-old king who is then described negotiating with Babylon, ruling in his own right, and being imprisoned for life reads as historically implausible; "eighteen" removes that difficulty and matches the parallel Kings account.
