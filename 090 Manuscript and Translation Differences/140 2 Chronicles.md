# 2 Chronicles: Significant Textual Variants Across 27 Translations

**Translations covered:** AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, RSV2CE, ASV, WEB, Douay-Rheims, CPDV, Darby, Young's Literal, Smith's Literal, Webster's Bible, AKJV, NRSV-CE, RSV, NABRE, ERV, NET, BSB, TRB, CJB

---

## 2 Chronicles 22:2 — was Ahaziah twenty-two, or forty-two, when he became king?

**The variant:** MT's Chronicles text gives Ahaziah's age as "forty-two." The parallel, textually stable account in 2 Kings 8:26 gives "twenty-two." A number of Hebrew manuscripts, the Septuagint, and the Syriac all read "twenty-two" at this verse in Chronicles as well. The "forty-two" reading additionally creates an internal contradiction within Chronicles itself: two verses earlier (21:20), Ahaziah's own father is said to have died at age 40 — making MT's Chronicles text have the son older than the father. Most scholars regard "forty-two" as a straightforward transmission error.

**How the translations render it:**
- **"Forty-two" (retaining MT's Chronicles reading as written):** KJV, NKJV. **Douay-Rheims, the RSV, and Darby** are all confirmed directly in this camp as well, along with **Smith's Literal Translation** ("The son of forty and two years was Ahaziah in his reigning"). **The CPDV is now confirmed directly** in this camp, matching the expectation ("Ahaziah was forty-two years old when he had begun to reign"). **The AKJV is also confirmed directly** in this camp ("Forty and two years old was Ahaziah when he began to reign"), matching KJV's wording exactly, as expected given its preservation method. **The WEB is also confirmed directly** in this camp ("Forty-two years old was Ahaziah when he began to reign") -- a genuinely notable result given WEB is a modern (1997) translation that here retains the harder Masoretic reading rather than joining the modern "twenty-two" harmonization most other contemporary translations adopt. **Webster's Bible is also confirmed directly** in this camp ("Forty and two years old was Ahaziah when he began to reign"), matching KJV's wording exactly.
- **"Twenty-two" (adopting the Septuagint/Syriac reading, harmonizing with 2 Kings 8:26):** ESV, NIV, NASB, CSB, NLT — generally with a footnote noting the Hebrew reads "forty-two." **ASV and the BSB** are both confirmed directly in this camp, along with **the ERV** ("He was 22 years old when he began to rule," with its own footnote marker acknowledging the textual issue) and **the NET Bible** ("Ahaziah was twenty-two years old when he became king"). **Young's Literal Translation is also confirmed directly** in this camp ("A son of twenty and two years") -- a genuinely notable result given YLT's own general reputation for extreme literalism and preference for the harder Masoretic reading elsewhere in this project, yet here it joins the harmonized reading instead. The **NRSV-CE** would be expected to follow the modern-critical-text pattern, though not separately confirmed. **AMP is also confirmed directly** in this camp, checked against its own text at blueletterbible.org: "Ahaziah was [fn]twenty-two years old when he became king," with a footnote reading "According to some versions and 2 Kin 8:26; Heb forty-two years, but this is most likely a copyist's error." **LSB is also confirmed directly** in this camp, checked against its own text at blueletterbible.org: "Ahaziah was [fn]twenty-two years old when he became king," with the same footnote convention as AMP.
- **Not independently confirmed in this research pass:** RSV2CE (as the original entry already noted), along with NABRE and CJB were not checked against their own text for this specific verse.

**The difference in meaning:** With "forty-two," the passage contains an internal chronological impossibility (a son older than his father). With "twenty-two," the passage is chronologically coherent and matches the parallel account in Kings.

## 2 Chronicles 36:9 — was Jehoiachin eight, or eighteen, when he became king?

**The variant:** MT's Chronicles text gives Jehoiachin's age as "eight." The parallel account in 2 Kings 24:8 gives "eighteen." A number of Hebrew manuscripts, plus Greek and Syriac witnesses, read "eighteen" at this verse in Chronicles too. The likely mechanical cause: the Hebrew word for "-teen" could easily drop out in copying, especially since a later corrective marginal note may have been misapplied instead to the number of days in his reign — which is why Chronicles specifies "three months and ten days" where Kings simply says "three months."

**How the translations render it:**
- **"Eight" (retaining MT's Chronicles reading as written):** KJV, NKJV. **Darby and Douay-Rheims** are both confirmed directly in this camp as well, along with **YLT** ("a son of eight years," explicitly noted by at least one commentator as the volume's own characteristically literal rendering) and **Smith's Literal Translation** ("The son of eight years was Jehoiachin in his reigning"). **The CPDV is now confirmed directly** in this camp, matching the expectation ("Jehoiachin was eight years old when he had begun to reign"). **The AKJV is also confirmed directly** in this camp ("Jehoiachin was eight years old"), matching KJV's wording exactly, as expected given its preservation method. **The WEB is also confirmed directly** in this camp ("Jehoiachin was eight years old when he began to reign"), matching the AKJV's wording almost exactly. **Webster's Bible is also confirmed directly** in this camp ("Jehoiachin was eight years old when he began to reign"), matching KJV's wording exactly. **NRSV-CE is also now confirmed directly** in this same camp, checked against its own published text at biblehub.com: "Jehoiachin was eight years old when he began to reign" -- a genuinely notable divergence from its own RSV cousin, since (as confirmed below) the RSV itself reads "eighteen" at this same verse, meaning NRSV-CE and RSV/RSV2CE land in opposite camps here despite their shared translation lineage.
- **"Eighteen" (adopting the Greek/Syriac/Hebrew-manuscript alternate, harmonizing with 2 Kings 24:8):** ESV, NASB, CSB, NIV, NLT. **ASV and the BSB** are both confirmed directly in this camp, along with **the NABRE** (confirmed directly via its official USCCB text) and **the RSV itself**, whose main text reads "eighteen" with a footnote explaining "Septuagint (compare 2 Kings 24:8); most Hebrew manuscripts eight years old." This RSV result is worth flagging specifically: at the companion Ahaziah verse above, the RSV instead retains the harder Masoretic "forty-two" -- meaning the RSV's translators made a verse-by-verse judgment here rather than applying one fixed rule favoring the Masoretic Text throughout, and RSV2CE's Old Testament, which generally follows this RSV base text, would accordingly be expected to read "eighteen" here despite reading "forty-two" at the Ahaziah verse. **The ERV is also confirmed directly** in this "eighteen" camp ("Jehoiachin was 18 years old when he became king of Judah"). **The NET Bible is also confirmed directly** in this camp ("Jehoiachin was eighteen years old when he became king"). **AMP is also confirmed directly** in this camp, checked against its own text at bibleproject.com, though with its own distinctive bracketed presentation directly signaling the harmonization within the text itself: "Jehoiachin was eight[teen] years old when he became king."
- **Not independently confirmed in this research pass:** AMP, LSB, and RSV2CE (as the original entry already noted), along with the CJB were not checked against their own text for this specific verse.

**The difference in meaning:** An 8-year-old king who is then described negotiating with Babylon, ruling in his own right, and being imprisoned for life reads as historically implausible; "eighteen" removes that difficulty and matches the parallel Kings account.
