# Numbers: Significant Textual Variants Across Ten Translations

**Translations covered:** AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, RSV2CE

The research pass behind this project did not confirm a case in Numbers where the ten tracked translations render a verse with different wording that changes its meaning at the tier covered elsewhere in this project. The items below are real questions in the wider manuscript and interpretive tradition, but this pass cannot confirm they produce a genuine meaning-changing split among the ten tracked translations.

---

## Side notes: cases in the wider tradition that don't clearly split the ten tracked translations

- **Numbers 22:5** — the Hebrew describing where Balaam was summoned from is textually ambiguous (it could be read as "the land of his people" or as a reference to the Ammonites), and translations vary somewhat in how they smooth this over in English. This is more a translation/interpretation difficulty with an unclear Hebrew phrase than a documented manuscript variant, so I'm not treating it as a confirmed meaning-changing split.
- **Numbers 24:7** — MT reads that the king Israel's coming ruler will be higher than "Agag" (a known Amalekite royal name, also appearing in 1 Samuel 15); the Septuagint and Samaritan Pentateuch instead read "Gog" (a name otherwise associated with a much later, apocalyptic figure in Ezekiel 38–39 and Revelation 20). All ten tracked translations follow MT's "Agag."
