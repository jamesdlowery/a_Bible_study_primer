# Numbers: Significant Textual Variants Across 27 Translations

**Translations covered:** AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, RSV2CE, ASV, WEB, Douay-Rheims, CPDV, Darby, Young's Literal, Smith's Literal, Webster's Bible, AKJV, NRSV-CE, RSV, NABRE, ERV, NET, BSB, TRB, CJB

The research pass behind this project did not confirm a case in Numbers where the tracked translations render a verse with different wording that changes its meaning at the tier covered elsewhere in this project. The items below are real questions in the wider manuscript and interpretive tradition, but this pass cannot confirm they produce a genuine meaning-changing split among the tracked translations.

---

## Side notes: cases in the wider tradition that don't clearly split the tracked translations

- **Numbers 22:5** — the Hebrew describing where Balaam was summoned from is textually ambiguous (it could be read as "the land of his people" or as a reference to the Ammonites), and translations vary somewhat in how they smooth this over in English. This is more a translation/interpretation difficulty with an unclear Hebrew phrase than a documented manuscript variant, so this is not treated as a confirmed meaning-changing split among any of the 27 tracked translations.
- **Numbers 24:7** — MT reads that the king Israel's coming ruler will be higher than "Agag" (a known Amalekite royal name, also appearing in 1 Samuel 15); the Septuagint and Samaritan Pentateuch instead read "Gog" (a name otherwise associated with a much later, apocalyptic figure in Ezekiel 38–39 and Revelation 20). The ten originally-tracked translations all follow MT's "Agag"; this was not independently re-checked across the additional 17, though no dissenting reading is expected given this is a well-established, stable MT reading.
