# Judges: Significant Textual Variants Across 27 Translations

**Translations covered:** AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, RSV2CE, ASV, WEB, Douay-Rheims, CPDV, Darby, Young's Literal, Smith's Literal, Webster's Bible, AKJV, NRSV-CE, RSV, NABRE, ERV, NET, BSB, TRB, CJB

---

## Judges 18:30 — was the idolatrous priest's grandfather "Moses," or "Manasseh"?

**The variant:** The Masoretic Text names the priest serving at the idol shrine in Dan as a grandson of "Manasseh" — but a small raised letter *nun* (ן) is inserted mid-word in the traditional Hebrew text, suspended above the line, which most textual scholars read as a scribal device signaling that the name was deliberately altered: remove that one letter, and the name reads "**Moses**" instead. The Septuagint, Vulgate, and some Hebrew manuscripts support the "Moses" reading directly. Most scholars conclude that later scribes found it uncomfortable to have a direct descendant of Moses serving at an idolatrous shrine, and inserted the extra letter specifically to change the name to the more distant, indirect "Manasseh" instead.

**How the translations render it:**
- **"Manasseh" (following the Masoretic Text as pointed):** KJV, NKJV, NASB (its predecessor ASV had read "Moses," but NASB's own translators switched back to "Manasseh").
- **"Moses" (following the Septuagint, Vulgate, and the evident original consonantal reading):** ESV, NIV, CSB, NLT, RSV2CE — most with a footnote explaining the suspended-letter device in the Hebrew. **ASV, Douay-Rheims, and RSV** are all confirmed directly in this camp as well, each reading "Moses" in their main text. Also confirmed directly in this camp: **Darby, the WEB, the NET Bible** (whose own translator's note explicitly states "several ancient textual witnesses, including some LXX mss. and the Vulgate, support the reading 'Moses' here"), and **the BSB**.
- **Not independently confirmed in this research pass:** AMP and LSB (as the original entry already noted), along with CPDV, Young's Literal, Smith's Literal, Webster's Bible, AKJV, NRSV-CE, NABRE, ERV, TRB, and CJB were not checked against their own text for this specific verse.

**The difference in meaning:** This is a genealogical claim with real weight — whether the man is a direct descendant of Moses himself (a striking and uncomfortable detail, given the idol-worship context) or of the more obscure "Manasseh." Nearly all modern textual scholars regard "Moses" as the original reading and "Manasseh" as a deliberate, theologically motivated alteration. Worth noting: the ASV's own reading ("Moses") did not survive into its direct descendant NASB, which reverted to "Manasseh" -- a useful reminder that even translations in the same family line can diverge on a specific verse when their editors reach different textual judgments.

## Judges 16:13–14 — a missing sentence in the Delilah loom scene

**The variant:** In the Masoretic Text, Delilah asks Samson how he could be bound, he tells her to weave his hair into a loom, and then — with no transition — the text jumps straight to Delilah doing exactly that, without ever narrating that she actually followed his instructions in between. This is a textbook case of **parablepsis** (a copyist's eye skipping from one occurrence of similar wording to a later one, dropping everything in between): the Septuagint preserves the missing sentence, in which Samson finishes his instructions ("...and fasten it with the pin, then I shall become weak like any other man") and Delilah is narrated as actually doing so before he falls asleep.

**How the translations render it:**
- **Restore the missing material in full, based on the Septuagint:** ESV, NIV, CSB, NLT.
- **Restore it but mark the added material as supplied (e.g., in brackets):** NASB.
- **Follow the shorter, elliptical Masoretic text as written, without supplying the missing sentence:** KJV, NKJV.
- **Not independently confirmed in this research pass:** AMP, LSB, and RSV2CE (as the original entry already noted), along with ASV, WEB, Douay-Rheims, CPDV, Darby, Young's Literal, Smith's Literal, Webster's Bible, AKJV, NRSV-CE, RSV, NABRE, ERV, NET, BSB, TRB, and CJB were not checked against their own text for this specific verse.

**The difference in meaning:** Without the restoration, the narrative has an actual gap — Delilah's action is narrated as if Samson's instructions were never repeated back or acted on step-by-step, which reads as abrupt in English. With the restoration, the scene flows as a complete, connected sequence. This is a case where most scholars agree the shorter reading reflects real, identifiable scribal data loss rather than a legitimate stylistic ellipsis.
