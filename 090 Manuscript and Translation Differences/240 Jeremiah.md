# Jeremiah: Textual and Translation Differences

**Translations covered:** AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, RSV2CE, ASV, WEB, DR, CPDV, Darby, YLT, SLT, WBT, AKJV, NRSV-CE, RSV, NABRE, ERV, NET, BSB, TRB, CJB

Jeremiah's most famous textual feature is a book-wide structural one, not a single-verse wording split, and the tracked translations all agree on which edition to follow. No separate verse was identified within Jeremiah where the tracked translations render the same words with a different, meaning-changing sense.

---

## Side note: a whole-book structural difference, not reflected among the tracked translations

- **Jeremiah, throughout** — the Septuagint's version of Jeremiah is roughly one-eighth shorter than the Masoretic Text and arranges some material (notably the oracles against foreign nations) in a substantially different order. A Dead Sea Scrolls Hebrew fragment (4QJerᵇ) confirms the shorter, reordered edition existed as a genuine independent Hebrew text, not just a loose Greek abridgment — making Jeremiah, alongside Samuel, one of the two clearest examples of two authentic literary editions of a Hebrew Bible book circulating in antiquity. All 27 tracked translations follow the longer Masoretic edition, in Masoretic order — including Douay-Rheims and the CPDV, whose Latin source (the Vulgate) was itself translated from the Hebrew. The shorter Septuagint edition is available in English only through direct translations of the Septuagint (such as Brenton's or the New English Translation of the Septuagint), none of which is among the translations tracked here.
