# Jeremiah: Textual and Translation Differences

**Translations covered:** AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, RSV2CE, ASV, WEB, DR, CPDV, Darby, YLT, SLT, WBT, AKJV, NRSV-CE, RSV, NABRE, ERV, NET, BSB, TRB, CJB

Jeremiah's most famous textual feature is a book-wide structural one, not a single-verse wording split, and the tracked translations all agree on which edition to follow. A systematic search did not find a separate verse within Jeremiah where the tracked translations render the same words with meaning-changing differences.

---

## Side note: a whole-book structural difference, not reflected among the tracked translations

- **Jeremiah, throughout** — the Septuagint's version of Jeremiah is roughly one-eighth shorter than the Masoretic Text and arranges some material (notably the oracles against foreign nations) in a substantially different order. A Dead Sea Scrolls Hebrew fragment (4QJerᵇ) confirms the shorter, reordered edition existed as a genuine independent Hebrew text, not just a loose Greek abridgment — making Jeremiah, alongside Samuel, one of the two clearest examples of two authentic literary editions of a Hebrew Bible book circulating in antiquity. All ten originally-tracked translations follow the longer Masoretic edition, in Masoretic order. This was not independently re-checked across the additional 17 translations in this research pass, though no dissenting translation is expected given how universally the Masoretic edition has been adopted by English translations generally, including those (like Douay-Rheims and the CPDV) working from an entirely different source language.
