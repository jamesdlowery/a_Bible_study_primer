# Leviticus: Textual and Translation Differences

**Translations covered:** AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, RSV2CE, ASV, WEB, Douay-Rheims, CPDV, Darby, Young's Literal, Smith's Literal, Webster's Bible, AKJV, NRSV-CE, RSV, NABRE, ERV, NET, BSB, TRB, CJB

Leviticus is one of the textually calmest books in the Old Testament, and the research pass behind this project did not find a case where the tracked translations actually render a verse with different wording that changes its meaning. The items below are real variants in the wider manuscript tradition, but none of them produces a split among the tracked translations.

---

## Side notes: cases where the tracked translations agree, but a real variant exists elsewhere in the manuscript tradition

- **Leviticus 17:3–4** — the Dead Sea Scrolls and Septuagint preserve a longer text specifying additional detail about slaughtering animals outside the camp; MT is shorter at this point. The ten originally-tracked translations all follow the shorter MT reading, and none flags the longer version in a footnote. This was not independently re-checked across the additional 17 translations in this research pass; given how minor and rarely discussed this variant is outside specialist textual-criticism literature, no dissenting English translation is expected, but this remains an unconfirmed expectation rather than a directly verified one.
- **Leviticus 18:21 / 20:2–5** — these verses concern child sacrifice "to Molech." A small number of Septuagint manuscripts show a scribal-level confusion between "Molech" (a god's name) and a similar-looking Hebrew word meaning something closer to "ruler" or "king," but this is generally regarded as an isolated Greek scribal error rather than a genuine competing Hebrew reading. The ten originally-tracked translations all render "Molech"; this was not independently re-checked across the additional 17, though no dissenting reading is expected given how isolated and minor this Greek-manuscript-only issue is.
