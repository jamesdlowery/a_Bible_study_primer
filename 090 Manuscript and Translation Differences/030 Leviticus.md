# Leviticus: Textual and Translation Differences

Leviticus is one of the textually calmest books in the Old Testament. No entry is recorded for Leviticus: no verse was identified where the 27 tracked translations render the same words with a different, meaning-changing sense. The absence of an entry marks the limit of the search, not proof that no such variant exists. The items below are real variants in the wider manuscript tradition, but none of them produces a split among the tracked translations.

## Side notes: cases where the tracked translations agree, but a real variant exists elsewhere in the manuscript tradition

- **Leviticus 17:3–4** — the Dead Sea Scrolls and Septuagint preserve a longer text specifying additional detail about slaughtering animals outside the camp; MT is shorter at this point. The ten translations tracked first (AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, and RSV2CE) all follow the shorter MT reading, and none flags the longer version in a footnote. The remaining 17 tracked translations (AKJV, ASV, BSB, CJB, CPDV, Darby, Douay-Rheims, ERV, NABRE, NET Bible, NRSV-CE, RSV, Smith's Literal Translation, TRB, WEB, Webster's Bible, and YLT) were not checked for this variant; given how minor and rarely discussed this variant is outside specialist textual-criticism literature, no dissenting English translation is expected.
- **Leviticus 18:21 / 20:2–5** — these verses concern child sacrifice "to Molech." A small number of Septuagint manuscripts show a scribal-level confusion between "Molech" (a god's name) and a similar-looking Hebrew word meaning something closer to "ruler" or "king," but this is generally regarded as an isolated Greek scribal error rather than a genuine competing Hebrew reading. The ten translations tracked first (AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, and RSV2CE) all render "Molech"; the remaining 17 tracked translations (AKJV, ASV, BSB, CJB, CPDV, Darby, Douay-Rheims, ERV, NABRE, NET Bible, NRSV-CE, RSV, Smith's Literal Translation, TRB, WEB, Webster's Bible, and YLT) were not checked, though no dissenting reading is expected given how isolated and minor this Greek-manuscript-only issue is.
