## Side note: a case where the tracked translations agree, but the variant is central to how three New Testament authors quote this verse

- **Habakkuk 2:4** — MT reads "the righteous shall live by **his** [i.e., the righteous person's own] faith/faithfulness." The Septuagint instead reads "the righteous shall live by **my** [i.e., God's] faith/faithfulness" — a one-word difference in whose faithfulness is in view, which most scholars attribute to a translation difficulty in the underlying Hebrew rather than a genuine competing Hebrew text. This matters because Paul quotes the verse in Romans 1:17 and Galatians 3:11, and the author of Hebrews quotes it in Hebrews 10:38 — in each case leaving out the possessive pronoun entirely ("the righteous shall live by faith"), sidestepping the his/my question altogether rather than resolving it. The ten translations tracked first (AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, and RSV2CE) all render Habakkuk 2:4 itself as "his faith" (or "his faithfulness"), following MT — none adopts the Septuagint's "my." So do the ASV, the CPDV, Darby, the Douay-Rheims Bible, the ERV, Smith's Literal Translation, Webster's Bible, and YLT, all of which likewise read "his faith" -- no dissenting translation was found among the 27 checked.
