# Habakkuk: Significant Textual Variants Across Ten Translations

**Translations covered:** AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, RSV2CE

A systematic search did not find a case in Habakkuk where the ten tracked translations render a verse with different, meaning-changing wording. The one well-known variant in this book, discussed below, is a case where all ten translations agree with each other, even though it has major New Testament significance.

---

## Side note: a case where all ten translations agree, but the variant is central to how three New Testament authors quote this verse

- **Habakkuk 2:4** — MT reads "the righteous shall live by **his** [i.e., the righteous person's own] faith/faithfulness." The Septuagint instead reads "the righteous shall live by **my** [i.e., God's] faith/faithfulness" — a one-word difference in whose faithfulness is in view, which most scholars attribute to a translation difficulty in the underlying Hebrew rather than a genuine competing Hebrew text. This matters because Paul quotes the verse in Romans 1:17 and Galatians 3:11, and the author of Hebrews quotes it in Hebrews 10:38 — in each case leaving out the possessive pronoun entirely ("the righteous shall live by faith"), sidestepping the his/my question altogether rather than resolving it. All ten tracked translations render Habakkuk 2:4 itself as "his faith" (or "his faithfulness"), following MT — none adopts the Septuagint's "my."
