# Amos: Textual and Translation Differences

**Translations covered:** AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, RSV2CE, ASV, WEB, Douay-Rheims, CPDV, Darby, Young's Literal, Smith's Literal, Webster's Bible, AKJV, NRSV-CE, RSV, NABRE, ERV, NET, BSB, TRB, CJB

A systematic search did not find a case in Amos where the tracked translations render a verse with different, meaning-changing wording. The one well-known variant in this book, discussed below, is a case where the tracked translations agree with each other.

---

## Side note: a case where the tracked translations agree, but the variant has real New Testament significance

- **Amos 9:11–12** — MT reads that Israel's restored kingdom will "possess the remnant of **Edom**." The Septuagint instead reads "the remnant of **mankind** ... may seek [the Lord]" — a real difference in the underlying Hebrew consonants. This matters because in Acts 15, James quotes the passage in its Septuagint form as scriptural support for admitting Gentiles into the church without circumcision. The ten originally-tracked translations all render Amos 9:12 itself following MT, "the remnant of Edom" — several (ESV, NIV, NASB) footnote the Septuagint reading and its use in Acts 15:17, but none adopts it into Amos's own main text. This was directly re-confirmed for the ASV, the Douay-Rheims Bible, the RSV, the BSB, and YLT, all of which likewise read "the remnant of Edom" -- no dissenting translation was found among the 27 checked.
