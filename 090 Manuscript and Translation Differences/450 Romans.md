# Romans: Significant Textual Variants Across 27 Translations

**Translations covered:** AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, RSV2CE, ASV, WEB, Douay-Rheims, CPDV, Darby, Young's Literal, Smith's Literal, Webster's Bible, AKJV, NRSV-CE, RSV, NABRE, ERV, NET, BSB, TRB, CJB

## 1. Romans 8:1 — a qualifying clause that grew over time

**The variant:** Paul's ringing declaration "There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus" is, in the earliest and best manuscripts, a clean, unqualified statement. But the majority of later Greek manuscripts (and so the Textus Receptus) add a qualifying phrase at the end of the verse: "...who do not walk according to the flesh, but according to the Spirit." Most textual scholars regard this addition as originally a scribal note copied in from verse 4 later in the same passage (where nearly identical wording legitimately appears, describing the outcome for those in Christ), which then migrated into verse 1 itself in the copying process — a well-documented type of scribal error where a phrase from a nearby verse gets duplicated into the wrong place.

**Why it matters:** Theologically, this is a meaningful difference. Without the added clause, verse 1 states no condemnation as an unconditional fact for anyone in Christ. With the clause, the verse reads more like a conditional promise — no condemnation *provided that* a person also walks according to the Spirit rather than the flesh — which shifts the verse from a declaration of a settled legal status to something closer to a description of ongoing behavior. Many theologians consider the shorter reading more consistent with Paul's argument in Romans, where the "no condemnation" of chapter 8 is generally understood to flow directly from the justification described in the preceding chapters, not from a believer's subsequent walk.

**Translations — a real split:**
- **Include the additional clause:** KJV and NKJV. **The AKJV and YLT** would be expected to include it, on the same Textus-Receptus-preservation grounds discussed throughout this NT section. **Smith's Literal Translation is also confirmed directly** in this full-inclusion camp ("no condemnation to them in Christ Jesus, walking not according to the flesh, but according to the Spirit"), joining KJV and NKJV rather than Douay-Rheims' distinct partial reading below. **The WEB is also confirmed directly** in this same full-inclusion camp ("who don't walk according to the flesh, but according to the Spirit"). **Webster's Bible is also confirmed directly** in this camp, matching KJV's wording exactly ("no condemnation to them who are in Christ Jesus, who walk not according to the flesh, but according to the Spirit").
- **Omit the clause, following the shorter and generally preferred earlier reading:** ESV, NIV, CSB, NASB, LSB, NLT, and RSV2CE (nearly all with a footnote noting that some manuscripts add the clause, sometimes citing its likely origin in verse 4). **The ERV is also confirmed directly** in this omitting camp ("So now anyone who is in Christ Jesus is not judged guilty"), with no trace of the disputed clause at all. **The BSB is also confirmed directly** in this omitting camp ("there is now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus"), with no trace of the disputed clause. **The ASV is also confirmed directly** in this omitting camp ("There is therefore now no condemnation to them that are in Christ Jesus"), with no trace of the disputed clause. **The NET Bible is also confirmed directly** in this omitting camp ("There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus"), with no trace of the disputed clause. **Darby is also confirmed directly** in this omitting camp ("[There is] then now no condemnation to those in Christ Jesus"), with no trace of the disputed clause.
- **A third, genuinely hybrid reading, confirmed directly and not previously documented for this entry:** **the Douay-Rheims Bible** reads "There is now therefore no condemnation to them that are in Christ Jesus, who walk not according to the flesh" -- including the first half of the disputed clause ("who walk not according to the flesh") while omitting the second half ("but according to the Spirit") entirely. This is neither the full Textus-Receptus reading nor the fully shortened modern-critical-text reading, but a distinct partial form reflecting the Vulgate's own specific wording at this verse -- a useful reminder that the pattern of Douay-Rheims simply "including" or "omitting" disputed material as a whole, documented at several other verses elsewhere in this project, does not hold uniformly; each verse needs its own direct check. **The CPDV is now confirmed directly**, matching Douay-Rheims' exact same hybrid pattern: "there is now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus, who are not walking according to the flesh" -- again including the first half of the clause while omitting "but according to the Spirit" entirely.
- **AMP** renders the shorter reading in its main text.
- **Not independently confirmed in this research pass:** NRSV-CE, RSV, NABRE, TRB, and the CJB were not checked against their own text for this specific verse.

## 2. Romans 16:24 — a closing grace-benediction before the doxology

**The variant:** Near the letter's end, a number of later Greek manuscripts (and so the Textus Receptus) include an additional benediction: "The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all. Amen." The verse is absent from the earliest and generally most highly regarded manuscripts, which move directly from verse 23 to the closing doxology (16:25–27). Most textual scholars regard verse 24 as a scribal repetition of the very similar benediction already given at 16:20 ("The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you"), duplicated here to provide a customary closing grace-wish immediately before the final doxology.

**Why it matters:** The sentiment is undisputed, since a nearly identical benediction already appears earlier in the same chapter — the question here is narrow: whether Paul's letter states it twice in close succession, or once.

**Translations — a real split:**
- **Include the verse in the main text, without qualification:** KJV, NKJV. **The AKJV and YLT** would be expected to include it, on the same grounds noted throughout this section. **Confirmed directly, and correcting this entry's prior assumption**, the Douay-Rheims Bible also includes this verse ("The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all! Amen") -- the fourteenth confirmed instance of Douay-Rheims including, rather than omitting, material absent from the earliest manuscripts. **Darby and the ASV are also confirmed directly** including it, each reading "The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all. Amen." **Smith's Literal Translation is also confirmed directly** including it, reading the same: "The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ with you all. Amen." **The WEB is also confirmed directly** including it, matching this same wording exactly: "The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all! Amen." **Webster's Bible is also confirmed directly** including it, matching KJV's wording exactly ("The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all. Amen").
- **Omit it from the main text, moving directly to the closing doxology, typically with a footnote noting its presence in some manuscripts:** ESV, NIV, CSB, NASB, LSB, NLT. **The ERV is also confirmed directly** in this omitting camp, using its characteristic minimal marking: its main text shows only "24 ‡" with no verse content, moving directly into verse 25. **The BSB is also confirmed directly** in this omitting camp, going even further: it omits the verse number entirely, moving directly from verse 23 into the doxology heading at verse 25. **The NET Bible is also confirmed directly** in this same fully-omitted style, moving directly from verse 23 into verse 25 with no "24" shown at all.
- **The CPDV is now confirmed directly** including this verse, matching the expectation ("The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all. Amen").
- **Not independently confirmed in this research pass:** AMP and RSV2CE (as the original entry already noted), along with NRSV-CE, RSV, NABRE, TRB, and the CJB were not checked against their own text for this specific verse.

## Summary observations

- Romans 8:1 is a good illustration of a specific, well-understood mechanism of scribal error (a phrase migrating in from a nearby verse) producing a variant with real theological weight, rather than a case driven by any doctrinal controversy.
- Both entries in this book follow the now-familiar textual-tradition pattern established throughout this project's New Testament section: KJV-preservation and Textus-Receptus-following translations on one side, critical-text and Vulgate-tradition translations on the other.
