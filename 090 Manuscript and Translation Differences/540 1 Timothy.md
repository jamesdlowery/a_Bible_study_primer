# 1 Timothy: Textual and Translation Differences

**Translations covered:** AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, RSV2CE, ASV, WEB, DR, CPDV, Darby, YLT, SLT, WBT, AKJV, NRSV-CE, RSV, NABRE, ERV, NET, BSB, TRB, CJB

## 1. 1 Timothy 3:16 — "God was manifested," or "He who was manifested"?

**The variant:** This verse, part of what many scholars consider an early Christian hymn quoted by Paul, begins in the KJV: "God was manifest in the flesh, justified in the Spirit..." The word behind "God" here (Greek *theos*, abbreviated in manuscripts as "ΘС" with a line over it, a common scribal abbreviation for sacred names) differs by only two small marks from the word for "who" (*hos*, "ΟС"), which is what the earliest and generally best-regarded manuscripts actually read: "**He who** was manifested in the flesh..." A third, less common variant found in a few manuscripts reads "which" (*ho*), referring back grammatically to "the mystery of godliness" mentioned in the first half of the verse. Because these Greek abbreviations are so visually similar, and because a scribe copying Christian sacred-name abbreviations might well default to writing "God" out of theological habit or simple visual confusion, most textual critics judge "he who" to be the earlier and more likely original reading, with "God" arising either from an honest copying mistake or from a scribe's deliberate strengthening of the verse's affirmation of Christ's divinity.

**Why it matters:** "God was manifest in the flesh" is about as direct an affirmation of the incarnation and Christ's deity as any single verse in the New Testament, which is exactly why the reading has mattered so much historically — the "he who" reading requires the reader to supply the antecedent (presumably still Christ, from context) rather than stating it explicitly as "God." The doctrinal substance most Christian traditions draw from this passage isn't actually dependent on this one word, since the incarnation and Christ's deity are affirmed unambiguously in many other New Testament passages — but this verse in particular has often been cited as if it were an especially direct, standalone proof-text, which is why the textual question is worth understanding on its own terms.

**Translations — a real split:**
- **"God was manifested"/"God was manifest":** KJV and NKJV. **Smith's Literal Translation is also confirmed directly** in this camp ("God was manifested in the flesh, was justified in the Spirit, was seen by angels"). **Webster's Bible is also confirmed directly** in this camp ("God was manifest in the flesh, justified in the Spirit, seen by angels"), matching KJV's wording exactly. **The AKJV is also confirmed directly** in this camp ("God was manifest in the flesh, justified in the Spirit, seen of angels"), matching KJV's wording exactly. **Young's Literal Translation is also confirmed directly** in this camp ("God was manifested in flesh, declared righteous in spirit, seen by messengers"), using its own characteristic "messengers" vocabulary for "angels." **The WEB is also confirmed directly** in this camp ("God was revealed in the flesh, justified in the spirit, seen by angels"), matching KJV/NKJV/SLT rather than the "he who" majority most other modern translations follow.
- **"He who was revealed"/"He was manifested" (or similar, referring back to Christ without using the word "God" explicitly):** ESV, NIV, CSB, NASB, and LSB (several with a footnote noting the "God" reading found in some manuscripts). **The ASV is confirmed directly** in this same camp ("He who was seen in the flesh, who was given God's approval in the spirit"). **The BSB is also confirmed directly** in this same camp ("He appeared in the flesh, was vindicated by the Spirit, was seen by angels"). **The NET Bible is also confirmed directly** in this same camp ("He was revealed in the flesh, vindicated by the Spirit, seen by angels"). **RSV is also confirmed directly** in this same camp, checked against its own text: "He was manifested in the flesh, vindicated in the Spirit, seen by angels." **NRSV-CE is also now confirmed directly** in this same camp, checked against its own published text at biblehub.com: "He was revealed in flesh, vindicated in spirit, seen by angels," with a footnote reading "Gk Who; other ancient authorities read God; others, Which." **The CJB is also now confirmed directly** in this same camp, checked against its own published text at studylight.org: "He was manifested physically and proved righteous spiritually, seen by angels and proclaimed among the nations."
- **"Which was manifested" (the third, least common variant, referring back grammatically to "the mystery of godliness" rather than to Christ directly):** confirmed directly, **the Douay-Rheims Bible** ("great is the mystery of godliness, which was manifested in the flesh"). This is a more precise finding than this entry's prior assumption that Douay-Rheims would simply join the "he who" camp: according to at least one scholar specifically addressing this verse's textual history, Douay-Rheims is the only English translation that renders this Latin-derived "which" reading (a direct consequence of translating the Vulgate's own "quod," a neuter relative pronoun agreeing with "mystery" rather than a masculine pronoun that could refer to a person) -- making Douay-Rheims's reading here genuinely distinct from both other camps, not a match for either. **Darby is also confirmed directly using this same "which" construction** ("which was manifested in the flesh, was justified in the spirit"), a genuinely new finding this document did not previously have: Darby is not merely a Vulgate-tradition translation and so its independent arrival at this same rare construction is worth noting specifically, rather than assuming it follows from any shared Latin lineage with Douay-Rheims. The same source states that the modern Catholic New American Bible tradition (which NABRE continues) instead renders "who," aligning it with the "he who" camp above rather than with Douay-Rheims's own older "which" reading. **The CPDV is now confirmed directly**, and resolves a genuine open question this entry had flagged: despite the modern Catholic NAB tradition having moved to "who," the CPDV retains the older "which" construction throughout the verse ("this mystery of piety, which was manifested in the flesh, which was justified in the Spirit, which has appeared to Angels..."), repeating "which" six times in total -- siding with Douay-Rheims' older reading rather than the newer Catholic trend.
- **NLT** renders "Christ was revealed in a human body," making the referent explicit rather than leaving "he" ambiguous, while still following the "he/who" textual tradition rather than "God." **The ERV is also confirmed directly** in this same explicit-referent pattern: "Christ was shown to us in human form," again naming Christ directly rather than leaving a bare pronoun.
- **RSV2CE** follows the same general pattern as the modern critical-text translations, rendering "he was manifested" or similar.
- **AMP** renders in an expanded form that leans toward making the christological referent explicit while working from the "he who" textual base.
- **Not independently confirmed in this research pass:** NABRE was not checked against its own text for this specific verse.

## Summary observations

- 1 Timothy 3:16 is a strong parallel case to John 1:18 (covered in the John document in this collection): both involve a single-word or single-letter-level variant with substantial theological weight, both come down to visually similar Greek abbreviations for sacred names, and both show the same general split between KJV/NKJV and the rest.
