# 1 Peter: Significant Textual Variants Across 27 Translations

**Translations covered:** AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, RSV2CE, ASV, WEB, Douay-Rheims, CPDV, Darby, Young's Literal, Smith's Literal, Webster's Bible, AKJV, NRSV-CE, RSV, NABRE, ERV, NET, BSB, TRB, CJB

---

## 1. 1 Peter 3:15 — "sanctify the Lord God," or "sanctify Christ as Lord"?

**The variant:** Instructing believers on how to face hostile questioning about their faith, the majority of later Greek manuscripts (and so the Textus Receptus) read "sanctify the Lord God in your hearts" (*Kyrion ton Theon*) — closely echoing the Old Testament language of Isaiah 8:13, "sanctify the LORD of hosts himself." The earliest and generally most highly regarded manuscripts instead read "sanctify Christ as Lord" (*Kyrion ton Christon*) — a one-word substitution (Christon for Theon) that directly applies the exact language Isaiah uses for God himself to Christ specifically.

**Why it matters:** Both readings affirm Christ's full deity elsewhere in the letter and in the New Testament generally, so no core doctrine is at stake either way — but the "Christ as Lord" reading, if original, is itself a notable, explicit instance of New Testament language directly transferring an Old Testament description of God onto Jesus, at exactly the point where Peter is alluding to Isaiah's own "sanctify the LORD" language.

**Translations — a real split:**
- **"Sanctify the Lord God":** KJV ("sanctify the Lord God in your hearts"), NKJV (same). **The AKJV, Webster's Bible, and YLT** would be expected to render "the Lord God" as well, on the same Textus-Receptus-preservation grounds discussed throughout this NT section.
- **"Sanctify Christ as Lord" / "regard Christ the Lord as holy":** ESV, NIV, CSB, NASB, LSB, NLT all render some form of "Christ as Lord" rather than "the Lord God," following the earlier manuscripts -- and confirmed directly, **the Douay-Rheims Bible** ("sanctify in your hearts Christ as Lord"), **Darby** ("sanctify the Lord Christ in your hearts"), **the BSB** ("sanctify [the] Lord the Christ in your hearts"), and **the ASV** ("sanctify in your hearts Christ as Lord," matching Douay-Rheims' exact phrasing). **The CPDV**, given Douay-Rheims' confirmed reading here, would be expected to follow the same "Christ as Lord" pattern, though its own text was not separately confirmed.
- **Not independently confirmed in this research pass:** AMP and RSV2CE (as the original entry already noted), along with WEB, Smith's Literal Translation, NRSV-CE, RSV, NABRE, the ERV, the NET Bible, TRB, and the CJB were not checked against their own text for this specific verse.
