## Side note: a case where the tracked translations agree, but the variant has attracted significant scholarly debate

- **Hebrews 2:9** — describing Christ's death, the well-attested reading is that he tasted death for everyone "by the grace of God" (*chariti theou*). A small number of manuscripts and patristic citations instead read "apart from God" (*chōris theou*) — a one-word difference that some scholars connect to early reflection on Jesus' cry of dereliction from the cross ("My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?"). The "apart from God" reading is generally judged poorly attested and secondary by modern textual critics. The ten translations tracked first (AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, and RSV2CE) all render "by the grace of God"; none was found to adopt "apart from God," even as a footnote. The remaining 17 tracked translations (AKJV, ASV, BSB, CJB, CPDV, Darby, Douay-Rheims, ERV, NABRE, NET Bible, NRSV-CE, RSV, Smith's Literal Translation, TRB, WEB, Webster's Bible, and YLT) were not checked for this variant, though no dissenting reading is expected given how poorly attested "apart from God" is even in the wider manuscript tradition.
