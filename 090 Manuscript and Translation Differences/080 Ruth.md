# Ruth: Textual and Translation Differences

Ruth, like Leviticus, is textually one of the calmest books in the Old Testament. No entry is recorded for Ruth: no verse was identified where the 27 tracked translations render the same words with a different, meaning-changing sense. The absence of an entry marks the limit of the search, not proof that no such variant exists.

---

## Side notes: cases in the wider tradition that don't split the tracked translations

- **Ruth 3:15** — a famous Kethiv/Qere-adjacent case: the earliest printed 1611 KJV read "he went into the city" (referring to Boaz), while the standard 1769 KJV revision (the text basis for virtually all KJVs printed today, and for NKJV) corrected this to "she went into the city" (referring to Ruth), matching the Hebrew Qere. The ten translations tracked first (AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, and RSV2CE) were reported as all reading "she"; on re-checking, that holds for nine of them but not the NIV, whose current text reads "Then he went back to town" (with a footnote), so even the first ten are split. Among the 17 added later, however, the split is live, checked against each translation's own text: ASV, BSB, Darby, Smith's Literal Translation, WEB, and YLT read "he went into the city" (the Hebrew as written; BSB footnotes that many Hebrew manuscripts, the Vulgate, and the Syriac read "she"), while AKJV, Webster's Bible, Douay-Rheims, the CPDV, and TRB read "she went into the city" -- so the "he" reading of the first 1611 printing survives today in the ASV-family and literalist translations rather than being a closed printing-history matter. The RSV (and RSV2CE, in the RSV Catholic Edition text it carries forward) reads "then she went into the city"; CJB, ERV, NABRE, NET Bible, and NRSV-CE were not checked for this variant.
- **Ruth 4:5** — the Hebrew as written (Kethiv) could be read as "I have acquired," but the traditional marginal correction (Qere) reads "you must also acquire" (referring to Boaz addressing the nearer kinsman-redeemer). The ten translations tracked first (AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, and RSV2CE) all follow the Qere, "you [the kinsman] must also acquire/buy"; of the 17 added later, AKJV, ASV, BSB, CPDV, Darby, Douay-Rheims, TRB, WEB, and Webster's Bible likewise follow the Qere ("thou must buy it also" / "you also acquire"), while YLT ("thou hast bought it") and Smith's Literal Translation ("thou boughtest it") follow the Kethiv, rendering the verb as a completed purchase rather than an obligation -- the only two tracked translations found doing so; CJB, ERV, NABRE, NET Bible, NRSV-CE, and RSV were not checked for this variant.
