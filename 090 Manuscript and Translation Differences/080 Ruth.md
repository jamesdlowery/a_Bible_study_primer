# Ruth: Textual and Translation Differences

**Translations covered:** AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, RSV2CE, ASV, WEB, DR, CPDV, Darby, YLT, SLT, WBT, AKJV, NRSV-CE, RSV, NABRE, ERV, NET, BSB, TRB, CJB

Ruth, like Leviticus, is textually one of the calmest books in the Old Testament. No entry is recorded for Ruth: no verse was identified where the 27 tracked translations render the same words with a different, meaning-changing sense. The absence of an entry marks the limit of the search, not proof that no such variant exists.

---

## Side notes: cases in the wider tradition that don't split the tracked translations

- **Ruth 3:15** — a famous Kethiv/Qere-adjacent case: the earliest printed 1611 KJV read "he went into the city" (referring to Boaz), while the standard 1769 KJV revision (the text basis for virtually all KJVs printed today, and for NKJV) corrected this to "she went into the city" (referring to Ruth), matching the Hebrew Qere. The ten translations tracked first (AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, and RSV2CE) all read "she," so there's no live split among them today — this is really a case of an older printing history being resolved rather than an ongoing manuscript variant. The remaining 17 tracked translations were not checked for this variant, though no dissenting reading is expected given this is a settled printing-history matter rather than a live manuscript question.
- **Ruth 4:5** — the Hebrew as written (Kethiv) could be read as "I have acquired," but the traditional marginal correction (Qere) reads "you must also acquire" (referring to Boaz addressing the nearer kinsman-redeemer). The ten translations tracked first (AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, and RSV2CE) all follow the Qere, "you [the kinsman] must also acquire/buy"; the remaining 17 tracked translations were not checked.
