# Daniel: Significant Textual Variants Across 27 Translations

**Translations covered:** AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, RSV2CE, ASV, WEB, Douay-Rheims, CPDV, Darby, Young's Literal, Smith's Literal, Webster's Bible, AKJV, NRSV-CE, RSV, NABRE, ERV, NET, BSB, TRB, CJB

As with Esther elsewhere in this collection, Daniel's most significant textual difference isn't a wording variant within a shared verse — it's the presence or absence of entire additional passages, tracking Catholic vs. Protestant/Jewish canon rather than manuscript preference.

---

## The Additions to Daniel — entire passages present in some tracked translations, absent from most

**The variant:** The Greek tradition of Daniel includes three passages with no counterpart in the Hebrew/Aramaic text: the Prayer of Azariah and Song of the Three Young Men (expanding the fiery-furnace episode in chapter 3), and two additional stories, Susanna and Bel and the Dragon (typically numbered as chapters 13 and 14). The Council of Trent affirmed this material as canonical for Roman Catholics.

**How the translations handle it:** As with the Esther Additions elsewhere in this collection, this divide tracks each translation's canon tradition directly.

- **Include the Additions as canonical text, integrated at their traditional points:** RSV2CE, confirmed as the original entry states. **Douay-Rheims, the CPDV, the NRSV-CE, and the NABRE** would all be expected to include the Additions as well, being Catholic-tradition translations that (like RSV2CE) affirm the Council of Trent's canon -- a reasoned expectation based on stated canonical scope, consistent with how the parallel Esther Additions entry treats the same set of translations.
- **Do not include this material:** AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, and NLT, as the original entry states. **ASV, WEB, Darby, Young's Literal, Smith's Literal, Webster's Bible, AKJV, the ERV, the NET Bible, the BSB, TRB, and the CJB** would all be expected to fall in this same camp as Protestant-tradition or (for CJB) Jewish-tradition translations. **The RSV** itself, in its standard (non-Catholic) edition, would also be expected to omit this material from its main canonical text.

**The difference in meaning:** As with Esther, this is a difference in how much material is in the book at all, not a wording difference within a shared passage. Comparing a Catholic-tradition Daniel against a Protestant or Jewish-tradition one means comparing a longer book (with two additional short stories and an extended prayer) against a shorter one, not two translations of the same text.
