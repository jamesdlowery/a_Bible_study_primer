# Ephesians: Significant Textual Variants Across 27 Translations

**Translations covered:** AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, RSV2CE, ASV, WEB, Douay-Rheims, CPDV, Darby, Young's Literal, Smith's Literal, Webster's Bible, AKJV, NRSV-CE, RSV, NABRE, ERV, NET, BSB, TRB, CJB

A systematic search did not find a case in Ephesians where the tracked translations render a verse with different, meaning-changing wording. The most famous variant in this letter, discussed below, is a case where the tracked translations agree with each other.

---

## Side note: a case where the tracked translations agree, but the variant has major implications for how the letter is understood

- **Ephesians 1:1** — the words "in Ephesus" are absent from several of the earliest manuscripts (Papyrus 46, the original hands of Sinaiticus and Vaticanus) and unknown to Origen and Basil of Caesarea. Without them, the verse simply reads "to the saints who are also faithful in Christ Jesus," with no named destination — unusual for an ancient letter opening, and part of why many scholars think Ephesians was originally a circular letter meant to be copied to multiple congregations with the destination filled in locally. All ten originally-tracked translations render "in Ephesus" in the main text (several with a footnote noting the omission in early manuscripts) — none adopts the shorter reading as its main text. This was not independently re-checked across the additional 17 translations in this research pass, though no dissenting translation is expected given how universally the "in Ephesus" reading has been retained even by translations otherwise closely following the earliest manuscripts elsewhere.
