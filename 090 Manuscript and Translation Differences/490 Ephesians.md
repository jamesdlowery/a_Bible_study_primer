# Ephesians: Textual and Translation Differences

No entry is recorded for Ephesians: no verse was identified where the 27 tracked translations render the same words with a different, meaning-changing sense. The most famous variant in this letter, discussed below, is a case where the tracked translations agree with each other.

## Side note: a case where the tracked translations agree, but the variant has major implications for how the letter is understood

- **Ephesians 1:1** — the words "in Ephesus" are absent from several of the earliest manuscripts (Papyrus 46, the original hands of Sinaiticus and Vaticanus) and unknown to Origen and Basil of Caesarea. Without them, the verse simply reads "to the saints who are also faithful in Christ Jesus," with no named destination — unusual for an ancient letter opening, and part of why many scholars think Ephesians was originally a circular letter meant to be copied to multiple congregations with the destination filled in locally. Of the ten translations tracked first (AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, and RSV2CE), nine render "in Ephesus" in the main text (several with a footnote noting the omission in early manuscripts); the **RSV2CE**, checked against its own text as read in the Ignatius Catholic Study Bible, complete edition (Ignatius Press, 2024), instead follows the RSV in omitting the words — "To the saints who are also faithful in Christ Jesus" — with the footnote "Other ancient authorities read who are at Ephesus and faithful" and a study note that "the addition 'at Ephesus' is doubtful. The letter may have been a form of encyclical." Of the 17 translations added later, AKJV, ASV, BSB, CPDV, Darby, Douay-Rheims, Smith's Literal Translation, TRB, WEB, Webster's Bible, and YLT were each checked against their own text and all retain "in Ephesus" (or "at Ephesus") in the main text, BSB with a footnote noting its absence from some manuscripts; among the six checked online, CJB, ERV, and NRSV-CE read "in Ephesus" in the main text, the NABRE and NET Bible print "[in Ephesus]" in square brackets to mark its doubtful status, and the RSV (with the RSV Catholic Edition) omits the words altogether, reading "To the saints who are also faithful in Christ Jesus" — so the RSV and RSV2CE are the only tracked translations adopting the shorter text.
