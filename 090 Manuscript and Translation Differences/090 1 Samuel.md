# 1 Samuel: Textual and Translation Differences

1 Samuel is textually one of the most interesting books in the Old Testament -- the Masoretic Text here is widely regarded as one of the least well-preserved in the Hebrew Bible, and the Dead Sea Scrolls (4QSamᵃ) recover real material. But in the two most-discussed cases below, the tracked translations actually agree with each other, so neither produces a live split. No entry is recorded for 1 Samuel: no verse was identified where the 27 tracked translations render the same words with a different, meaning-changing sense. The absence of an entry marks the limit of the search, not proof that no such variant exists.

---

## Side notes: real variants in the wider tradition that don't split the tracked translations

- **1 Samuel 10:27–11:1** — a Dead Sea Scrolls fragment (4QSamᵃ), corroborated by Josephus, preserves an entire paragraph explaining that Nahash the Ammonite had already been systematically gouging out the right eye of Israelites across the Jordan — background missing from both MT and the Septuagint, which agree with each other here against the Qumran scroll. The ten translations tracked first (AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, and RSV2CE) all follow the shorter MT/LXX text, without this paragraph; of the 17 added later, AKJV, ASV, BSB, CPDV, Darby, Douay-Rheims, Smith's Literal Translation, TRB, WEB, Webster's Bible, and YLT were each checked against their own text and all print the shorter text without the paragraph (BSB footnotes the Qumran reading in full); Douay-Rheims and the CPDV do open 11:1 with "about a month after," the Vulgate's reflection of the Greek's opening phrase, which the Qumran paragraph also contains, but neither includes the Nahash material itself; CJB, ERV, NABRE, NET Bible, NRSV-CE, and RSV were not checked for this variant.
- **1 Samuel 17:4** — MT gives Goliath's height as "six cubits and a span" (roughly 9'9"); the Septuagint's major manuscripts, a Dead Sea Scrolls fragment (4QSamᵃ), and Josephus all instead give "four cubits and a span" (roughly 6'9"). The ten translations tracked first (AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, and RSV2CE) all follow MT's "six cubits." So do the Douay-Rheims Bible, the RSV, the BSB, and the CJB, all of which likewise read "six cubits" (or its equivalent) in their main text -- no English translation checked renders "four cubits" in its main text, though several (including the ESV) note the shorter reading in a footnote.
