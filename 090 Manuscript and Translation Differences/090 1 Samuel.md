# 1 Samuel: Textual and Translation Differences

1 Samuel is textually one of the most interesting books in the Old Testament -- the Masoretic Text here is widely regarded as one of the least well-preserved in the Hebrew Bible, and the Dead Sea Scrolls (4QSamᵃ) recover real material. But in the two most-discussed cases below, the tracked translations actually agree with each other, so neither produces a live split. No entry is recorded for 1 Samuel: no verse was identified where the 27 tracked translations render the same words with a different, meaning-changing sense. The absence of an entry marks the limit of the search, not proof that no such variant exists.

---

## Side notes: real variants in the wider tradition that don't split the tracked translations

- **1 Samuel 10:27–11:1** — a Dead Sea Scrolls fragment (4QSamᵃ), corroborated by Josephus, preserves an entire paragraph explaining that Nahash the Ammonite had already been systematically gouging out the right eye of Israelites across the Jordan — background missing from both MT and the Septuagint, which agree with each other here against the Qumran scroll. Of the ten translations tracked first (AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, and RSV2CE), nine follow the shorter MT/LXX text without this paragraph, while the NLT prints the paragraph at the end of 10:27 in square brackets, with a footnote; of the 17 added later, AKJV, ASV, BSB, CPDV, Darby, Douay-Rheims, Smith's Literal Translation, TRB, WEB, Webster's Bible, and YLT were each checked against their own text and all print the shorter text without the paragraph (BSB footnotes the Qumran reading in full); Douay-Rheims and the CPDV do open 11:1 with "about a month after," the Vulgate's reflection of the Greek's opening phrase, which the Qumran paragraph also contains, but neither includes the Nahash material itself. Among the critical-text translations the picture is more varied: **the NRSV-CE**, like its NRSV parent, adopts the Qumran paragraph into the main text as verse 10:27b ("Now Nahash, king of the Ammonites, had been grievously oppressing the Gadites and the Reubenites..."), and **the ERV** likewise prints the paragraph in its main text, without brackets, at the end of 10:27 ("King Nahash of the Ammonites had been hurting the tribes of Gad and Reuben..."); **the NABRE** keeps the shorter text but opens 11:1 with the Greek's "About a month later" and summarizes the Qumran report in a footnote; **the NET Bible** keeps the shorter text and argues in its note that the paragraph may preserve a text superior to the Masoretic one; and **the RSV** (1952, predating the Qumran publication) has the shorter text. The CJB keeps the shorter text. One further caution: the WEB text supplied for this check lacks the paragraph, but at least one current online WEB edition prints it in square brackets at 10:27, so the WEB's own editions appear to differ here and the reading above reflects the edition consulted.
- **1 Samuel 17:4** — MT gives Goliath's height as "six cubits and a span" (roughly 9'9"); the Septuagint's major manuscripts, a Dead Sea Scrolls fragment (4QSamᵃ), and Josephus all instead give "four cubits and a span" (roughly 6'9"). The ten translations tracked first (AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, and RSV2CE) all follow MT's "six cubits." So do the Douay-Rheims Bible, the RSV, the BSB, and the CJB, all of which likewise read "six cubits" (or its equivalent) in their main text -- no English translation checked renders "four cubits" in its main text, though several (including the ESV) note the shorter reading in a footnote.
