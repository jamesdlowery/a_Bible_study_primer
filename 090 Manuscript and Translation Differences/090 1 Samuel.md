# 1 Samuel: Significant Textual Variants Across Ten Translations

**Translations covered:** AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, RSV2CE

1 Samuel is textually one of the most interesting books in the Old Testament -- the Masoretic Text here is widely regarded as one of the least well-preserved in the Hebrew Bible, and the Dead Sea Scrolls (4QSamᵃ) recover real material. But in the two most-discussed cases below, all ten tracked translations actually agree with each other, so neither produces a live split among the ten. A systematic search did not find a separate case in 1 Samuel where the ten tracked translations render a verse with different, meaning-changing wording.

---

## Side notes: real variants in the wider tradition that don't split the ten tracked translations

- **1 Samuel 10:27–11:1** — a Dead Sea Scrolls fragment (4QSamᵃ), corroborated by Josephus, preserves an entire paragraph explaining that Nahash the Ammonite had already been systematically gouging out the right eye of Israelites across the Jordan — background missing from both MT and the Septuagint, which agree with each other here against the Qumran scroll. All ten tracked translations follow the shorter MT/LXX text, without this paragraph.
- **1 Samuel 17:4** — MT gives Goliath's height as "six cubits and a span" (roughly 9'9"); the Septuagint's major manuscripts, a Dead Sea Scrolls fragment (4QSamᵃ), and Josephus all instead give "four cubits and a span" (roughly 6'9"). All ten tracked translations follow MT's "six cubits."
