# 1 Samuel: Significant Textual Variants Across 27 Translations

**Translations covered:** AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, RSV2CE, ASV, WEB, Douay-Rheims, CPDV, Darby, Young's Literal, Smith's Literal, Webster's Bible, AKJV, NRSV-CE, RSV, NABRE, ERV, NET, BSB, TRB, CJB

1 Samuel is textually one of the most interesting books in the Old Testament -- the Masoretic Text here is widely regarded as one of the least well-preserved in the Hebrew Bible, and the Dead Sea Scrolls (4QSamᵃ) recover real material. But in the two most-discussed cases below, the tracked translations actually agree with each other, so neither produces a live split. A systematic search did not find a separate case in 1 Samuel where the tracked translations render a verse with different, meaning-changing wording.

---

## Side notes: real variants in the wider tradition that don't split the tracked translations

- **1 Samuel 10:27–11:1** — a Dead Sea Scrolls fragment (4QSamᵃ), corroborated by Josephus, preserves an entire paragraph explaining that Nahash the Ammonite had already been systematically gouging out the right eye of Israelites across the Jordan — background missing from both MT and the Septuagint, which agree with each other here against the Qumran scroll. The ten originally-tracked translations all follow the shorter MT/LXX text, without this paragraph; not independently re-checked across the additional 17 translations in this research pass, though no dissenting English translation is expected given this material is uniquely attested only in a single Qumran fragment and Josephus.
- **1 Samuel 17:4** — MT gives Goliath's height as "six cubits and a span" (roughly 9'9"); the Septuagint's major manuscripts, a Dead Sea Scrolls fragment (4QSamᵃ), and Josephus all instead give "four cubits and a span" (roughly 6'9"). The ten originally-tracked translations all follow MT's "six cubits." This was directly re-confirmed for the Douay-Rheims Bible, the Revised Standard Version, the Berean Standard Bible, and the Complete Jewish Bible, all of which likewise read "six cubits" (or its equivalent) in their main text -- no English translation checked in this research pass renders "four cubits" in its main text, though several (including the ESV, among the ten originally tracked) note the shorter reading in a footnote.
