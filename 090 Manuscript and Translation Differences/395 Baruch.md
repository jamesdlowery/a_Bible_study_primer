# Baruch (including the Letter of Jeremiah): Textual and Translation Differences

**Comparison translations:** RSV2CE, NABRE, DR, and CPDV.

No entry is recorded for Baruch: no verse was identified where RSV2CE, NABRE, Douay-Rheims, and the CPDV render the same words with a different, meaning-changing sense. The absence of an entry marks the limit of the search, not proof that no such variant exists.

---

## Background

Baruch is a composite of several originally separate works (an opening confession of sin, a Wisdom poem, laments and consolations addressed to Jerusalem, and the Letter of Jeremiah appended as chapter 6), likely composed originally in Hebrew for at least the earlier material, though no Hebrew manuscript of Baruch itself survives — only a small Greek fragment of the Letter of Jeremiah portion has turned up among the Dead Sea Scrolls (7Q2). All the English Catholic translations in common use (RSV2CE, NABRE, Douay-Rheims, and by extension the CPDV) present Baruch as a single continuous book with the same basic structure, so the kind of large-scale divergence seen in Tobit or Judith is not expected here; whether smaller wording differences exist has not been checked verse by verse.
