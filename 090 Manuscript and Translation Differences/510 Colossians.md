# Colossians: Significant Textual Variants Across 27 Translations

**Translations covered:** AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, RSV2CE, ASV, WEB, Douay-Rheims, CPDV, Darby, Young's Literal, Smith's Literal, Webster's Bible, AKJV, NRSV-CE, RSV, NABRE, ERV, NET, BSB, TRB, CJB

## Colossians 1:14 — redemption "through his blood," or simply "redemption"?

**The variant:** Describing what believers have in Christ, the majority of later Greek manuscripts (and so the Textus Receptus) read "in whom we have redemption **through his blood**, the forgiveness of sins." The earliest and most significant manuscripts lack the phrase "through his blood" entirely, reading simply "in whom we have redemption, the forgiveness of sins." Most textual scholars regard "through his blood" as a scribal harmonization, copied in from the nearly identical wording of the parallel verse, Ephesians 1:7, which does contain "through his blood" in every manuscript tradition without dispute.

**How the translations render it:**
- **Include "through his blood":** KJV ("through his blood, even the forgiveness of sins"), NKJV ("through His blood, the forgiveness of sins"). **Confirmed directly**, the Douay-Rheims Bible includes the phrase as well ("In whom we have redemption through his blood, the remission of sins") -- the eighth confirmed instance in this project's set of disputed-verse entries (see the companion Matthew, Mark, Luke, John, Acts, and Romans entries elsewhere in this document set) of Douay-Rheims siding with the longer, Textus-Receptus-aligned reading at this general category of variant. **Darby is also confirmed directly** including the phrase ("In whom we have redemption through his blood, the remission of sins"). **Smith's Literal Translation is also confirmed directly** ("In whom we have redemption by his blood, the remission of sins"). **The AKJV and YLT** would be expected to include the phrase as well, on the same Textus-Receptus-preservation grounds discussed throughout this NT section. **Webster's Bible is also confirmed directly**, matching KJV's wording exactly ("In whom we have redemption through his blood, even the forgiveness of sins"). **AMP is also confirmed directly** in this camp, checked against multiple independent sources: "In Whom we have our redemption through His blood, [which means] the forgiveness of our sins."
- **Omit it, following the earlier and generally preferred shorter reading:** ESV, NIV, CSB, NASB, LSB, NLT — most with a footnote noting that some manuscripts add the phrase, based on Ephesians 1:7. **The ASV is confirmed directly** in this omitting camp as well ("in whom we have our redemption, the forgiveness of our sins," with no "through his blood" phrase at all). **The ERV is also confirmed directly** in this omitting camp, with its own paraphrase avoiding "blood" language entirely: "The Son paid the price to make us free. In him we have forgiveness of our sins." **The WEB is also confirmed directly** in this omitting camp ("in whom we have our redemption, the forgiveness of our sins"), with no "through his blood" phrase. **The BSB is also confirmed directly** in this omitting camp ("in whom we have redemption, the forgiveness of sins"), with no "through his blood" phrase. **The NET Bible is also confirmed directly** in this same camp, with identical wording to BSB ("in whom we have redemption, the forgiveness of sins"). **RSV is also confirmed directly** in this omitting camp, checked against its own text: "in whom we have redemption, the forgiveness of sins," with no "through his blood" phrase. **RSV2CE is also now confirmed directly** in this same camp, checked against its own published text at biblia.com, using identical wording to its RSV parent: "in whom we have redemption, the forgiveness of sins." **NRSV-CE is also now confirmed directly** in this same camp, checked against its own published text at biblehub.com: "in whom we have redemption, the forgiveness of sins," with a footnote reading "Other ancient authorities add through his blood." **NABRE is also now confirmed directly** in this same camp, checked against its own official text at bible.usccb.org: "in whom we have redemption, the forgiveness of sins," with no "through his blood" phrase.
**The CPDV is now confirmed directly** including the phrase, matching the expectation ("in whom we have redemption through his blood, the remission of sins").
- **Not independently confirmed in this research pass:** TRB was not checked against its own text for this specific verse. **The CJB is also now confirmed directly** in the omitting camp above, checked against its own published text at biblestudytools.com: "It is through his Son that we have redemption -- that is, our sins have been forgiven," with no "through his blood" phrase.

**The difference in meaning:** Both readings affirm redemption and forgiveness through Christ; the shorter reading simply doesn't specify the means ("blood") explicitly at this particular point, leaving that detail to be understood from context and from parallel passages like Ephesians 1:7 rather than stated twice.
