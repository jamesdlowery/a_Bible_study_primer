# Colossians: Textual and Translation Differences

---

## Colossians 1:14 — redemption "through his blood," or simply "redemption"?

**The variant:** Describing what believers have in Christ, the majority of later Greek manuscripts (and so the Textus Receptus) read "in whom we have redemption **through his blood**, the forgiveness of sins." The earliest and most significant manuscripts lack the phrase "through his blood" entirely, reading simply "in whom we have redemption, the forgiveness of sins." Most textual scholars regard "through his blood" as a scribal harmonization, copied in from the nearly identical wording of the parallel verse, Ephesians 1:7, which does contain "through his blood" in every manuscript tradition without dispute.

**How the translations render it:**
- **Include "through his blood":** KJV ("through his blood, even the forgiveness of sins"), NKJV ("through His blood, the forgiveness of sins"). The Douay-Rheims Bible includes the phrase as well ("In whom we have redemption through his blood, the remission of sins") -- the eighth confirmed instance in this book's set of disputed-verse entries (see the companion Matthew, Mark, Luke, John, Acts, and Romans entries elsewhere in this chapter set) of Douay-Rheims siding with the longer, Textus-Receptus-aligned reading at this general category of variant. **Darby** also includes the phrase ("In whom we have redemption through his blood, the remission of sins"). **Smith's Literal Translation** is also in this camp ("In whom we have redemption by his blood, the remission of sins"). **The AKJV and YLT** were not checked for this verse but would be expected to include the phrase as well, on the same Textus-Receptus-preservation grounds discussed throughout this NT section. **Webster's Bible** also matches KJV's wording exactly ("In whom we have redemption through his blood, even the forgiveness of sins"). **AMP** is also in this camp: "In Whom we have our redemption through His blood, [which means] the forgiveness of our sins."
- **Omit it, following the earlier and generally preferred shorter reading:** ESV, NIV, CSB, NASB, LSB, NLT — most with a footnote noting that some manuscripts add the phrase, based on Ephesians 1:7. **ASV** is in this omitting camp as well ("in whom we have our redemption, the forgiveness of our sins," with no "through his blood" phrase at all). **ERV** is also in this omitting camp, with its own paraphrase avoiding "blood" language entirely: "The Son paid the price to make us free. In him we have forgiveness of our sins." **WEB** is also in this omitting camp ("in whom we have our redemption, the forgiveness of our sins"), with no "through his blood" phrase. **BSB** is also in this omitting camp ("in whom we have redemption, the forgiveness of sins"), with no "through his blood" phrase. **NET Bible** is also in this same camp, with identical wording to BSB ("in whom we have redemption, the forgiveness of sins"). **RSV** is also in this omitting camp: "in whom we have redemption, the forgiveness of sins," with no "through his blood" phrase. **RSV2CE** is also in this same camp, using identical wording to its RSV parent: "in whom we have redemption, the forgiveness of sins." **NRSV-CE** is also in this same camp: "in whom we have redemption, the forgiveness of sins," with a footnote reading "Other ancient authorities add through his blood." **NABRE** is also in this same camp: "in whom we have redemption, the forgiveness of sins," with no "through his blood" phrase.
**CPDV** also includes the phrase ("in whom we have redemption through his blood, the remission of sins").
- **CJB** is also in the omitting camp above: "It is through his Son that we have redemption -- that is, our sins have been forgiven," with no "through his blood" phrase.
- **TRB** brackets the phrase: "in whom we have redemption [through his blood], the forgiveness of sins."

**The difference in meaning:** Both readings affirm redemption and forgiveness through Christ; the shorter reading simply doesn't specify the means ("blood") explicitly at this particular point, leaving that detail to be understood from context and from parallel passages like Ephesians 1:7 rather than stated twice.
