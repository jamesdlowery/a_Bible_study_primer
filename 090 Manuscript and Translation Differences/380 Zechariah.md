# Zechariah: Textual and Translation Differences

Zechariah has two well-known candidate cruxes, described below. Both are documented below, including a genuinely three-way split at 11:13.

---

## Zechariah 11:13 — "potter," "treasury," or something else entirely?

**The variant:** MT has the prophet told to throw his wages "to the potter" (*hayotser*) in the house of the LORD; this is the verse Matthew 27:9-10 draws on for the "potter's field" purchased with Judas's returned silver. The Syriac Peshitta instead reads "treasury" (*ha'otsar*) — a plausible one-consonant confusion between the two similar-looking Hebrew words.

**How the translations render it:**
- **"Potter":** KJV, NIV, NASB, CSB, NLT. **Webster's Bible, the WEB, and YLT** are also in this camp, along with **NKJV** ("Throw it to the potter"), **the ASV** ("Cast it unto the potter"), and **Smith's Literal Translation** ("Cast it to the potter... cast it in the house of Jehovah, to the potter"). **AKJV** is also in this camp ("Cast it to the potter... cast them to the potter in the house of the LORD"), matching KJV's wording exactly, as expected given its preservation method. **BSB** is also in this camp ("Throw it to the potter... threw them to the potter in the house of the LORD"). **NET Bible** is also in this camp ("Throw to the potter that exorbitant sum at which they valued me!... threw them to the potter at the temple of the LORD"). **Darby** is also in this camp ("Cast it unto the potter... cast them to the potter in the house of Jehovah"). **AMP** is also in this camp: "Cast it to the potter [as if He said, To the dogs!]... And I took the thirty pieces of silver and cast them to the potter in the house of the Lord."
- **"Treasury":** **ESV, the RSV, and the NRSV (and, by direct extension, the NRSV-CE)** are also in this camp — notable because ESV in particular departs from the "potter" reading found in most of the other tracked translations. **CJB** is also in this camp ("Throw it into the treasury!"), along with **the ERV** ("Throw that large amount of money into the Temple treasury"). **RSV2CE** is also in this same camp, using identical wording to its RSV parent: "Cast it into the treasury—the lordly price at which I was paid off by them. So I took the thirty shekels of silver and cast them into the treasury in the house of the Lord."
- **A third, distinct reading — "statuary":** the **Douay-Rheims Bible** renders this "cast it to the statuary" (a caster or molder of images), neither "potter" nor "treasury" but a related craft-term reflecting the Vulgate's own specific Latin wording. **Catholic Public Domain Version** uses this same wording ("I cast them into the house of the Lord, towards the statuary"), matching Douay-Rheims exactly as expected given its general reliance on that translation.
- **Not verified:** LSB and TRB were not checked against their own text for this verse.

**The difference in meaning:** "Potter" ties the verse directly to the Matthew 27 "potter's field" narrative that quotes it; "treasury" removes that specific connection, describing instead a straightforward act of returning the money to the temple's general funds; "statuary" (Douay-Rheims) sits closer to "potter" in general sense (both describe a craftsman who works with molded material) without matching either English word choice directly.

## Zechariah 12:10 — "whom they have pierced," or "because they have mocked"?

**The variant:** MT has the people looking "on me, whom they have pierced" (widely read as messianic and quoted in John 19:37); the Septuagint instead reads something closer to "look on me because they have mocked/danced insultingly," reflecting a different Hebrew verb.

**How the translations render it:** This turns out to be a case of essentially universal agreement, not a live split. **KJV, RSV, Douay-Rheims, and ASV** render "whom they have pierced" (or very close equivalents), matching the other tracked translations. The Septuagint's "mocked" reading does not appear to be adopted as the main-text reading by any mainstream English translation checked -- it survives only in direct English translations of the Septuagint text itself (e.g., Brenton's translation), not in any translation of the Hebrew MT-based tradition. No dissenting translation was found among the 27 checked.
