# Jude: Significant Textual Variants Across Ten Translations

**Translations covered:** AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, RSV2CE

---

## 1. Jude 1:5 — did "the Lord," or "Jesus," save the people out of Egypt?

**The variant:** Recalling Old Testament examples of God's judgment, Jude reminds his readers of one they already know: that "the Lord" (or, in a different reading, "Jesus") having saved the people out of the land of Egypt, afterward destroyed those who did not believe. The Textus Receptus and most later manuscripts read "the Lord" (*ho Kyrios*); a number of the earliest and most highly regarded manuscripts instead read "Jesus" (*Iēsous*) — a genuinely striking reading, since it would have Jude describing the pre-incarnate Christ as the one who led Israel out of Egypt and later judged the unbelieving generation in the wilderness. The current standard critical edition of the Greek New Testament (Nestle-Aland 28th edition, 2012) changed its own preferred main-text reading to "Jesus," reversing the previous edition's preference for "the Lord" — making this one of the more consequential recent shifts in mainstream New Testament textual scholarship.

**Why it matters:** If "Jesus" is original, this is among the more direct New Testament statements identifying Jesus with the God of Israel active throughout the Old Testament narrative, not merely present or foreshadowed — a substantive, if not doctrine-altering, difference in how explicitly this one verse makes that identification.

**Translations — a real split:**
- **"The Lord":** KJV ("the Lord, having saved the people out of the land of Egypt"), NKJV (same).
- **"Jesus":** ESV renders "Jesus, who saved a people out of the land of Egypt," explicitly following the reading adopted in NA28; CSB and NIV likewise read "Jesus" in their main text.
- I don't have solid, separately verified data for AMP, NASB, LSB, NLT, and RSV2CE on this specific verse — several translations' most recent editions have revisited this reading following NA28's 2012 change, so I'd rather flag this as unverified than guess which side of the split each currently falls on.
