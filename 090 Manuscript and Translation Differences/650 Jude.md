# Jude: Textual and Translation Differences

---

## 1. Jude 1:5 — did "the Lord," or "Jesus," save the people out of Egypt?

**The variant:** Recalling Old Testament examples of God's judgment, Jude reminds his readers of one they already know: that "the Lord" (or, in a different reading, "Jesus") having saved the people out of the land of Egypt, afterward destroyed those who did not believe. The Textus Receptus and most later manuscripts read "the Lord" (*ho Kyrios*); a number of the earliest and most highly regarded manuscripts instead read "Jesus" (*Iēsous*) — a genuinely striking reading, since it would have Jude describing the pre-incarnate Christ as the one who led Israel out of Egypt and later judged the unbelieving generation in the wilderness. The current standard critical edition of the Greek New Testament (Nestle-Aland 28th edition, 2012) changed its own preferred main-text reading to "Jesus," reversing the previous edition's preference for "the Lord" — making this one of the more consequential recent shifts in mainstream New Testament textual scholarship.

**Why it matters:** If "Jesus" is original, this is among the more direct New Testament statements identifying Jesus with the God of Israel active throughout the Old Testament narrative, not merely present or foreshadowed — a substantive, if not doctrine-altering, difference in how explicitly this one verse makes that identification.

**Translations — a real split:**
- **"The Lord":** KJV ("the Lord, having saved the people out of the land of Egypt"), NKJV (same). **The AKJV** was not checked for this verse but would be expected to render "the Lord" as well, on the same Textus-Receptus-preservation grounds discussed throughout this section. Also in this "the Lord" camp: **Darby** ("the Lord, having saved the people out of the land of Egypt"), **Webster's Bible** (same wording), **YLT**, and **the ASV**, per multiple sources cataloguing this specific split -- and, **the WEB** ("that the Lord, having saved a people out of the land of Egypt, afterward destroyed those who didn't believe") and **Smith's Literal Translation** ("that the Lord, having saved the people out of the land of Egypt, the second time destroyed them not having believed"). **ERV** is also in this camp: "Remember that the Lord saved his people by bringing them out of the land of Egypt," with its own footnote marker acknowledging the textual issue. **The NRSV is also reported in this camp** (as distinct from the newer NRSVUE revision, which has since changed to "Jesus" -- a genuine editorial shift between NRSV editions worth distinguishing, since this book tracks NRSV specifically, not NRSVUE), and by direct extension **the NRSV-CE** was not checked for this verse but would be expected to follow the same NRSV-family reading.
- **"Jesus":** ESV renders "Jesus, who saved a people out of the land of Egypt," explicitly following the reading adopted in NA28; CSB and NIV (2011 revision) likewise read "Jesus" in their main text. **The NET Bible is also reported reading "Jesus"** in this same camp. **Both the Douay-Rheims Bible and the CPDV read "Jesus" here as well** -- Douay-Rheims: "that Jesus, having saved the people out of the land of Egypt, did afterwards destroy them that believed not"; the CPDV: "Those who once knew everything that Jesus did, in saving the people from the land of Egypt, afterwards perished because they did not believe." **BSB** is also in this camp ("after Jesus had delivered His people out of the land of Egypt, He destroyed those who did not believe"). This is not a coincidence of independent modern scholarship converging with an ancient Catholic translation -- the Latin Vulgate itself, which both Douay-Rheims and the CPDV translate, has long read "Iesus" ("Jesus") at this verse, centuries before NA28's 2012 shift toward the same reading in the Greek critical tradition. This means the "Jesus" reading was never actually absent from the major translation traditions surveyed in this chapter; it was simply confined, until recently, to the Vulgate-derived translations rather than the Greek-based ones. **LSB** is also in this camp: "that Jesus, having once saved a people out of the land of Egypt, subsequently destroyed those who did not believe" -- a genuinely notable divergence from its own NASB parent text, which (as confirmed below) reads "the Lord" rather than "Jesus" at this same verse, meaning LSB's translators made an independent textual choice here rather than simply following NASB's own reading.
- **A third, genuinely distinct reading:** **the RSV itself is reported reading "he"** rather than either "the Lord" or "Jesus" -- reflecting a conjecture by the textual scholar F. J. A. Hort that the original Greek may have read a bare demonstrative pronoun (transliterated *ho*), from which both the "Lord" and "Jesus" readings could plausibly have developed as later scribal expansions supplying a name. Given the RSV's own confirmed choice here, **RSV2CE's specific wording at this verse is worth checking directly** rather than assumed to follow either the "Lord" or "Jesus" camp, since this book has documented several cases elsewhere (see the companion 2 Chronicles entries) where RSV2CE and its RSV parent text diverge at specific verses.
- **A genuine, unresolved discrepancy worth flagging rather than guessing at: NABRE's position here is reported inconsistently across sources consulted.** One source (addressing this verse specifically in light of the 2012 NA28 shift) lists "NAB" among the translations reading "Jesus"; a separate source instead lists "NAB" among those reading "the Lord." This may reflect the two sources referring to different NAB editions (the original 1970 NAB versus the current 2011 NABRE revision), a distinction the sources themselves do not make explicit, or it may reflect a genuine error in one of them. This chapter does not resolve which is correct and does not guess at NABRE's actual current-edition reading here.
- **Not verified:** TRB was not checked against its own text for this verse. **NASB** is also in this camp (including its own text at biblehub.com/nasb): the current NASB reads "the Lord, after saving a people out of the land of Egypt, subsequently destroyed those who did not believe" — joining the "the Lord" camp with KJV and NKJV, not "Jesus." **NLT** also includes its own text at biblehub.com/nlt: "that Jesus first rescued the nation of Israel from Egypt, but later he destroyed those who did not remain faithful" — joining the "Jesus" camp with ESV and NET. **AMP** is also in this camp: "Now I want to remind you, although you are fully informed once for all, that the Lord, after saving a people out of the land of Egypt, subsequently destroyed those who did not believe [who refused to trust and obey and rely on Him]" — joining the "the Lord" camp with KJV, NKJV, and NASB. **CJB** is also in this same "the Lord" camp: "Adonai, who once delivered the people from Egypt, later destroyed those who did not trust."
