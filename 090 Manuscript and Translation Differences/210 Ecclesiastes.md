# Ecclesiastes: Significant Textual Variants Across Ten Translations

**Translations covered:** AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, RSV2CE

A systematic search did not find anything in Ecclesiastes that rises to the tier of significance covered elsewhere in this collection -- a genuine, well-documented manuscript variant (MT vs. Samaritan Pentateuch, Septuagint, or Dead Sea Scrolls) that changes meaning and that shows up as a real difference among the ten tracked translations.

Ecclesiastes is textually one of the more stable books of the Hebrew Bible (a fragment among the Dead Sea Scrolls, 4QQoheleth, is generally consistent with the later Masoretic Text). Its genuine difficulties for translators are almost entirely about interpreting the book's distinctive, sometimes ambiguous Hebrew idiom and philosophical vocabulary (most famously, how to render *hevel* -- "vapor" or "breath," traditionally "vanity," and rendered variously as "meaningless," "futile," or "fleeting" across the ten tracked translations) rather than about the manuscripts disagreeing on what the underlying text says. That's a real and substantive translation question, but it isn't a textual variant in the sense used throughout this project, so this entry is left as a placeholder rather than manufacturing an entry to match the format used elsewhere.
