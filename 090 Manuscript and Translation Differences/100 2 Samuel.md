# 2 Samuel: Significant Textual Variants Across 27 Translations

**Translations covered:** AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, RSV2CE, ASV, WEB, Douay-Rheims, CPDV, Darby, Young's Literal, Smith's Literal, Webster's Bible, AKJV, NRSV-CE, RSV, NABRE, ERV, NET, BSB, TRB, CJB

---

## 2 Samuel 21:19 — did Elhanan kill Goliath, or Goliath's brother?

**The variant:** MT's text of 2 Samuel 21:19, read literally, states that a warrior named Elhanan "struck down Goliath the Gittite." The parallel account in 1 Chronicles 20:5 instead says Elhanan struck down "Lahmi, the brother of Goliath the Gittite." Comparing the Hebrew consonants involved, most textual scholars conclude this is a traceable scribal slip in the transmission of Samuel specifically: the word for "the brother of" (*achi*) and a nearby word appear to have become garbled, with a portion of the text being absorbed into what should have read "Lahmi." Chronicles is generally judged to preserve the more original sense.

**How the translations render it:**
- **Supply "the brother of," harmonizing with 1 Chronicles 20:5:** KJV and NKJV (italicized to flag it as supplied, not a literal rendering of the Hebrew word-for-word), and also **NIV** (2011 revision) and **NLT**, both of which print "the brother of Goliath" plainly in their main text. **The WEB, Webster's Bible, and the BSB are all confirmed directly in this camp** as well. **YLT is confirmed directly including "brother of," but marks it explicitly with brackets** ("smiteth [a brother of] Goliath the Gittite") to flag it as supplied rather than a literal rendering of the bare Hebrew — a middle position between the unqualified KJV/NKJV inclusion and the translations that omit it. **The AKJV** would be expected to include it as well, given its KJV-preservation method.
- **Render literally, "killed" or "struck down Goliath the Gittite," leaving the apparent tension with 1 Samuel 17 unresolved in the text itself:** ESV, NASB, CSB, and also **AMP** (which prints the literal reading without any bracketed harmonizing note), **LSB** (which reads "struck down Goliath the Gittite," consistent with its general practice of tracking NASB's wording), and **RSV2CE** (which renders "slew Goliath the Gittite," following the Masoretic wording without supplying "brother of"). **The ASV and the RSV are both confirmed directly in this camp**, each reading "slew Goliath the Gittite" without qualification.
- **A third, wholly distinct reading:** the **Douay-Rheims Bible** departs from both camps entirely, rendering the warrior's name as "Adeodatus" rather than "Elhanan," and reading "slew Goliath the Gethite" without any mention of a brother at all — reflecting the Vulgate's own distinct textual and naming tradition at this verse, unconnected to either the "brother of" harmonization or the literal Hebrew-based "Goliath" reading found elsewhere. **The CPDV** would be expected to follow this same distinctive Vulgate-based reading, though not separately confirmed.
- **Not independently confirmed in this research pass:** Darby, Smith's Literal Translation, NRSV-CE, NABRE, the ERV, the NET Bible, TRB, and the CJB were not checked against their own text for this specific verse.

**The difference in meaning:** With "the brother of" supplied, the verse creates no conflict with 1 Samuel 17's account of David killing Goliath. Rendered literally, the verse appears (on a surface reading) to credit Elhanan with killing Goliath himself — the frequently-cited "Bible contradiction" example. Both readings describe the same underlying historical claim once Chronicles is factored in; the difference is whether the English translation resolves the ambiguity for the reader or leaves it for them to reconcile. Douay-Rheims' distinct naming tradition adds a further wrinkle specific to the Vulgate line, unconnected to either side of the main Hebrew-text-based debate.
