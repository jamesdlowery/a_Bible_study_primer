# 2 Samuel: Significant Textual Variants Across 27 Translations

**Translations covered:** AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, RSV2CE, ASV, WEB, Douay-Rheims, CPDV, Darby, Young's Literal, Smith's Literal, Webster's Bible, AKJV, NRSV-CE, RSV, NABRE, ERV, NET, BSB, TRB, CJB

---

## 2 Samuel 21:19 — did Elhanan kill Goliath, or Goliath's brother?

**The variant:** MT's text of 2 Samuel 21:19, read literally, states that a warrior named Elhanan "struck down Goliath the Gittite." The parallel account in 1 Chronicles 20:5 instead says Elhanan struck down "Lahmi, the brother of Goliath the Gittite." Comparing the Hebrew consonants involved, most textual scholars conclude this is a traceable scribal slip in the transmission of Samuel specifically: the word for "the brother of" (*achi*) and a nearby word appear to have become garbled, with a portion of the text being absorbed into what should have read "Lahmi." Chronicles is generally judged to preserve the more original sense.

**How the translations render it:**
- **Supply "the brother of," harmonizing with 1 Chronicles 20:5:** KJV and NKJV (italicized to flag it as supplied, not a literal rendering of the Hebrew word-for-word), and also **NIV** (2011 revision) and **NLT**, both of which print "the brother of Goliath" plainly in their main text. **The WEB, Webster's Bible, and the BSB are all confirmed directly in this camp** as well. **YLT is confirmed directly including "brother of," but marks it explicitly with brackets** ("smiteth [a brother of] Goliath the Gittite") to flag it as supplied rather than a literal rendering of the bare Hebrew — a middle position between the unqualified KJV/NKJV inclusion and the translations that omit it. **Darby is also confirmed directly in this camp** ("slew the brother of Goliath the Gittite"). **The AKJV** would be expected to include it as well, given its KJV-preservation method.
- **Render literally, "killed" or "struck down Goliath the Gittite," leaving the apparent tension with 1 Samuel 17 unresolved in the text itself:** ESV, NASB, CSB, and also **AMP** (which prints the literal reading without any bracketed harmonizing note), **LSB** (which reads "struck down Goliath the Gittite," consistent with its general practice of tracking NASB's wording), and **RSV2CE** (which renders "slew Goliath the Gittite," following the Masoretic wording without supplying "brother of"). **The ASV and the RSV are both confirmed directly in this camp**, each reading "slew Goliath the Gittite" without qualification. **The NABRE is also confirmed in this camp**, per at least one source specifically addressing this verse's textual history, which groups NABRE together with RSV2CE and the Jerusalem Bible as modern Catholic translations that "follow the Hebrew literally... and let the difficulty stand," rather than supplying the Chronicles harmonization.
- **A third, wholly distinct reading:** the **Douay-Rheims Bible** departs from both camps entirely, rendering the warrior's name as "Adeodatus" rather than "Elhanan," and reading "slew Goliath the Gethite" without any mention of a brother at all — reflecting the older Clementine Vulgate's own distinct textual and naming tradition at this verse, unconnected to either the "brother of" harmonization or the literal Hebrew-based "Goliath" reading found elsewhere. This specific naming does not survive into the modern official Catholic Vulgate: the 1979 Nova Vulgata reads "Elchanan" rather than "Adeodatus," meaning even within the Catholic Latin textual tradition this naming has since been revised. **The CPDV** would be expected to follow the older, Douay-Rheims-aligned "Adeodatus" reading rather than the Nova Vulgata's more recent "Elchanan," given CPDV's own stated basis in the Challoner Douay-Rheims tradition specifically, though this was not separately confirmed against CPDV's own published text.
- **Not independently confirmed in this research pass:** Smith's Literal Translation, NRSV-CE, the ERV, the NET Bible, TRB, and the CJB were not checked against their own text for this specific verse.

**The difference in meaning:** With "the brother of" supplied, the verse creates no conflict with 1 Samuel 17's account of David killing Goliath. Rendered literally, the verse appears (on a surface reading) to credit Elhanan with killing Goliath himself — the frequently-cited "Bible contradiction" example. Both readings describe the same underlying historical claim once Chronicles is factored in; the difference is whether the English translation resolves the ambiguity for the reader or leaves it for them to reconcile. Douay-Rheims' distinct naming tradition adds a further wrinkle specific to the Vulgate line, unconnected to either side of the main Hebrew-text-based debate.
