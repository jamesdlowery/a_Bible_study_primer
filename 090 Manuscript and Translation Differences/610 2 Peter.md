# 2 Peter: Textual and Translation Differences

2 Peter's textual base is comparatively stable outside one verse, but that verse is among the best-known cruxes in the New Testament, and the tracked translations divide over it cleanly.

---

## 2 Peter 3:10 — will the earth and its works be "burned up," or "found" / "exposed"?

**The variant:** At the end of 3:10, the Byzantine tradition and the Textus Receptus read *katakaēsetai*, "will be burned up," and this is the reading the KJV tradition and most translations before the late twentieth century follow. The two great fourth-century uncials, Sinaiticus and Vaticanus, read *heurethēsetai*, "will be found," which is the harder reading -- it is not obvious what it means for the earth and its works to "be found" -- and which modern critical editions (Nestle-Aland, UBS) adopt on the principle that a scribe would more readily replace the puzzling "found" with the expected "burned up" than the reverse. A few witnesses have other attempts at the difficult text (the Sahidic Coptic and one Syriac tradition read "will not be found"; Papyrus 72 adds a word yielding "will be found dissolved"), which is itself evidence that early copyists found "found" difficult. Modern translations that follow the critical text render *heurethēsetai* as "exposed," "laid bare," or "disclosed," taking the sense to be that the earth's works will be exposed to judgment.

**How the translations render it:**
- **"Burned up," following the Byzantine / Textus Receptus reading:** KJV ("the earth also and the works that are therein shall be burned up"), NKJV, AKJV, and Webster's Bible; the ASV, the WEB, and Darby; Young's Literal ("shall be burnt up"); the Douay-Rheims Bible ("shall be burnt up") and the CPDV; the NASB and the LSB ("will be burned up"); the AMP ("will be burned up"); and the RSV and RSV2CE ("will be burned up") -- notable, since the RSV otherwise follows the critical text closely, and its committee judged "found" too obscure to print.
- **"Found" / "exposed" / "laid bare," following Sinaiticus and Vaticanus:** ESV ("the earth and the works that are done on it will be exposed"), NIV ("will be laid bare"), CSB ("will be disclosed"), NRSV-CE ("will be disclosed"), NABRE ("will be found out"), BSB ("will be laid bare"), and the NET Bible ("will be laid bare"), the NET's own note explaining the textual choice at length.
- **Not verified:** NLT, ERV, SLT, and CJB were not checked against their own text for this verse and are not assigned to a camp here; NLT and ERV, as meaning-based translations working from the critical text, would be expected to fall in the second camp, but that is an expectation rather than a checked reading.
- **TRB** is in the "exposed" camp: "the earth and the works done in it will be exposed," with a footnote: "Some manuscripts: 'burned up.' The Greek text is uncertain."

**The difference in meaning:** "Burned up" makes the verse a straightforward prophecy of the earth's destruction by fire, and it is the wording behind most popular preaching on the passage. "Found" or "exposed" makes it a prophecy of judgment -- the earth and everything done on it laid open before God -- and leaves room for the renewal of creation rather than its annihilation, a distinction that matters in discussions of the new heavens and new earth (3:13). This is one of the clearest cases in the tracked set where a manuscript decision, not a translation philosophy, produces two different theological emphases; the KJV-tradition and the critical-text translations part ways here for exactly the reason the Method chapter describes, and the RSV's decision to stay with "burned up" shows that even a critical-text translation can decline the harder reading when it judges it unintelligible in English.
