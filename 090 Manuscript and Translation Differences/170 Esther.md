# Esther: Significant Textual Variants Across 27 Translations

**Translations covered:** AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, RSV2CE, ASV, WEB, Douay-Rheims, CPDV, Darby, Young's Literal, Smith's Literal, Webster's Bible, AKJV, NRSV-CE, RSV, NABRE, ERV, NET, BSB, TRB, CJB

This isn't a single-verse wording variant, but it's the largest and most consequential textual difference affecting Esther among the tracked translations, so it's called out here rather than as a side note.

---

## The Additions to Esther — an entire block of material present in some tracked translations, absent from most

**The variant:** The Hebrew Masoretic Text of Esther never mentions God, prayer, or explicit religious practice at all. A Greek edition of Esther that began circulating in the last two centuries BC adds six substantial passages — roughly 107 additional verses, nearly the length of the Hebrew book itself — including Mordecai's prophetic dream, the full text of the royal edicts, and explicit prayers by Mordecai and Esther invoking God by name. The Council of Trent (1546) affirmed the whole book, Hebrew text plus Greek additions, as canonical for Roman Catholics.

**How the translations handle it:** This divide tracks each translation's canon tradition directly, rather than requiring verse-by-verse comparison.

- **Include the Additions, as part of the translation's canonical text of Esther:** RSV2CE, confirmed as the original entry states. **Douay-Rheims, the CPDV, the NRSV-CE, and the NABRE** would all be expected to include the Additions as well, since each represents a Catholic-tradition canon that (like RSV2CE) affirms the Council of Trent's inclusion of these passages -- this is a reasoned expectation based on each translation's stated canonical scope rather than a verse-by-verse confirmation of every printed edition.
- **Contain only the Hebrew-based text, without this material:** AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, and NLT, as the original entry states. **ASV, WEB, Darby, Young's Literal, Smith's Literal, Webster's Bible, AKJV, the Easy-to-Read Version, the NET Bible, the Berean Standard Bible, The Readable Bible, and the Complete Jewish Bible** would all be expected to fall in this same camp, being Protestant-tradition or (for CJB) Jewish-tradition translations that do not affirm the Additions as canonical -- again a reasoned expectation based on canonical tradition rather than a separate check of every edition. **The RSV** itself, in its standard (non-Catholic) edition, would also be expected to omit the Additions from its main canonical text, though RSV editions that include the wider Apocrypha as a separate section do exist as a distinct print option.

**The difference in meaning:** This isn't a wording difference within a shared verse — it's the presence or absence of entire passages. The Hebrew-only Esther is a story where God's involvement is implied only through timing and circumstance; the version with the Additions is a book with explicit prayers and direct statements that God is acting. If you compare a Catholic-tradition Esther against a Protestant or Jewish-tradition one side by side, you're comparing two different lengths of the same core story, not two translations of identical material.
