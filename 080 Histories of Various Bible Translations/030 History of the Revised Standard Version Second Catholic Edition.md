# History of the Revised Standard Version Second Catholic Edition

**The Revised Standard Version, Second Catholic Edition (RSV-2CE), also known as the Ignatius Bible, is a carefully updated Catholic edition of the Revised Standard Version published by Ignatius Press in 2006.** It builds upon the earlier RSV Catholic Edition (RSV-CE) of 1965–1966 while introducing revisions that remove remaining archaic language, conform more closely to the principles of the Vatican instruction *Liturgiam authenticam* (2001), and restore certain traditional Catholic renderings. The RSV-2CE has become a favored translation among many English-speaking Catholics who prefer a formal, essentially literal style rooted in the classic English Bible tradition.

What follows places the RSV-2CE in the longer history of the Revised Standard Version and the Catholic editions that derived from it.

## The RSV and the First Catholic Edition

The Revised Standard Version (RSV) of 1946–1952 was a revision of the American Standard Version of 1901, continuing the formal-equivalence lineage that descends from the King James Version. In the 1960s, the Catholic Biblical Association of Great Britain prepared a Catholic Edition of the RSV. The RSV-CE New Testament appeared in 1965 and the complete Bible (with deuterocanonical books placed in traditional Catholic order) in 1966. The editors made only minimal changes to the RSV text, seeking “to make the minimum number of alterations, and to change only what seemed absolutely necessary in the light of Catholic tradition.”

The RSV-CE gained a loyal following among Catholic scholars, clergy, and lay readers who appreciated its dignity, accuracy, and continuity with the classic English Bible. It was later used in the English edition of the *Catechism of the Catholic Church*.

## Reasons for a Second Catholic Edition

By the early twenty-first century several factors prompted a further revision. The original RSV still retained some archaic pronouns and verb forms (“thee,” “thou,” “didst,” etc.) when addressing God. In addition, the 2001 Vatican instruction *Liturgiam authenticam* set clearer norms for translations intended for liturgical use, favoring greater fidelity to the original texts and to traditional Catholic terminology. Finally, the appearance of the more inclusive-language New Revised Standard Version (NRSV) in 1989 led many traditional-minded Catholics to prefer a version that remained closer to the RSV’s formal style while addressing the remaining archaisms.

Ignatius Press, long a champion of the RSV-CE, undertook the work of preparing a second Catholic edition.

## The 2006 Revision (RSV-2CE)

Published in early 2006, the RSV-2CE incorporates a number of deliberate changes:

- Removal of remaining archaic second-person singular pronouns and verb forms in favor of contemporary English (“you,” “your,” etc.).
- Adjustments made in light of *Liturgiam authenticam*.
- Restoration of certain traditional Catholic renderings. Notable examples include:
 - Isaiah 7:14: “virgin” (rather than “young woman”)
 - Luke 1:28: “full of grace” (rather than “O favored one”)
 - John 1:18 and parallel passages: restoration of “begotten”
 - Consistent use of “mercy” for the Hebrew *ḥesed* in many places in the Psalms (instead of “steadfast love”)

The deuterocanonical books remain in their traditional Catholic order. The edition carries the same imprimatur lineage as the original RSV-CE and was approved under that authority.

## Reception and Use

The RSV-2CE has been warmly received by many Catholics who value a translation that is both modern enough for clear reading and traditional enough to preserve the theological and literary resonance of earlier English Bibles. It is frequently recommended by Catholic apologists, theologians, and study-Bible editors (including those associated with the Ignatius Catholic Study Bible). It has been approved for liturgical use in the Personal Ordinariates for former Anglicans.

While the New American Bible Revised Edition remains the basis of the lectionary for most Roman Rite dioceses in the United States, the RSV-2CE continues to serve as a preferred text for personal study, lectio divina, and academic work among those who favor its style and textual choices.

## Place in the Larger Tradition

The Revised Standard Version, Second Catholic Edition stands as a careful refinement of the mid-twentieth-century RSV within a Catholic framework. It preserves the formal elegance and essential literalism of the RSV tradition while removing obstacles of archaic language and aligning selected renderings more closely with longstanding Catholic usage and recent Vatican norms for translation. In doing so, it offers English-speaking Catholics a dignified, accurate, and theologically sensitive modern Bible that remains recognizably in continuity with the great English versions that preceded it.
