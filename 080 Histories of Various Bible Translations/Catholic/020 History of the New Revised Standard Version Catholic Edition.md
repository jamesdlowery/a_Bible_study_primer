# History of the New Revised Standard Version Catholic Edition

**The New Revised Standard Version Catholic Edition (NRSV-CE) is the Catholic adaptation of the New Revised Standard Version (NRSV) of 1989.** It includes the complete Catholic canon of seventy-three books, with the deuterocanonical books placed in their traditional order within the Old Testament, and carries the imprimatur of the United States and Canadian Conferences of Catholic Bishops (granted in 1991). Because Catholic scholars participated in the original NRSV translation committee, the text of the shared books required no substantive alteration for Catholic use.

What follows places the NRSV-CE in the longer lineage of the Revised Standard Version tradition and traces its specific development, approval, and use within the Catholic Church.

## Background: From RSV to NRSV

The Revised Standard Version (RSV) of 1946–1952 was itself a revision of the American Standard Version of 1901, continuing the formal-equivalence tradition that descends from the King James Version. A Catholic Edition of the RSV (RSV-CE) was prepared by the Catholic Biblical Association of Great Britain and published in 1965–1966. It made only minimal changes to the RSV text while rearranging the deuterocanonical books according to Catholic canonical order. The RSV-CE became a respected scholarly and devotional text among English-speaking Catholics and was later used in the English edition of the *Catechism of the Catholic Church*.

In 1974 the National Council of the Churches of Christ in the U.S.A. authorized a full revision of the RSV to take account of further manuscript discoveries (including fuller use of the Dead Sea Scrolls), advances in linguistics, and changes in English usage. The resulting New Revised Standard Version appeared in 1989. Its translation philosophy has been summarized as “as literal as possible, as free as necessary.” The committee included Protestant, Catholic, Orthodox, and Jewish scholars.

## The Catholic Edition

Because Catholic scholars had already taken part in the NRSV work, the preparation of a Catholic Edition required chiefly the inclusion and proper ordering of the deuterocanonical books (Tobit, Judith, Wisdom, Sirach, Baruch, 1 and 2 Maccabees, and the longer forms of Esther and Daniel). Books accepted by some other traditions but not by the Catholic Church (such as 1 and 2 Esdras and the Prayer of Manasseh) were omitted. No significant changes were made to the text of the protocanonical books.

The NRSV-CE received the imprimatur of the United States Conference of Catholic Bishops and the Canadian Conference of Catholic Bishops in 1991, authorizing it for private study and devotional reading. An Anglicized-text edition, reflecting British spelling and usage, has also been published.

## Liturgical Status and Later Developments

While the NRSV-CE is approved for personal use and study, most English-speaking Catholic bishops’ conferences have not adopted it as the primary text for the lectionary at Mass. In the United States the New American Bible (and its revisions) remains the basis of the lectionary; elsewhere the Jerusalem Bible or other approved versions are commonly used. Canada, however, received Vatican recognition for a modified form of the NRSV for lectionary use beginning in 2008.

A further update of the underlying NRSV, the New Revised Standard Version Updated Edition (NRSVue), appeared in 2021. A Catholic Edition of the NRSVue has more recently received an imprimatur, continuing the lineage.

## Character and Reception

The NRSV-CE inherits the scholarly reputation of the NRSV: careful attention to the original languages, gender-accurate language where the Hebrew or Greek is inclusive, and a clear, contemporary English style that remains relatively formal. It has been widely used in Catholic academic settings, seminaries, and by individuals seeking a modern translation that includes the full Catholic canon in traditional order. Some prefer the more traditional language of the RSV-CE (especially the second Catholic edition published by Ignatius Press) or the specifically American Catholic scholarship of the New American Bible Revised Edition. The NRSV-CE continues to serve as a major ecumenical and Catholic resource for study of the Scriptures.

## Place in the Larger Tradition

The New Revised Standard Version Catholic Edition stands at the intersection of two important streams: the long Protestant revision tradition that runs from the King James Version through the Revised Version, American Standard Version, and Revised Standard Version, and the modern Catholic commitment to translations from the original languages encouraged by *Divino afflante Spiritu* and the Second Vatican Council. By combining rigorous scholarship, contemporary English, and the complete Catholic canon, the NRSV-CE offers English-speaking Catholics a reliable and widely respected modern Bible for study and devotion.
