# History of the Amplified Bible

**The Amplified Bible (AMP) is a distinctive English translation produced by The Lockman Foundation in partnership with Zondervan.** First completed as a one-volume Bible in 1965, it is best known for its system of amplifications—bracketed and parenthetical expansions that supply additional shades of meaning, synonyms, or clarifying phrases drawn from the original Hebrew, Aramaic, and Greek. The work began with the vision of Frances E. Siewert and remains one of the more unique approaches to English Bible translation in the twentieth and twenty-first centuries.

What follows traces its origins, development, major editions, and place among modern translations.

## Origins and Frances Siewert

Frances E. Siewert (1881–1967), a dedicated Christian educator and the widow of a Presbyterian minister, conceived the idea of an amplified New Testament in the early 1950s. Though she had limited formal training in the biblical languages, she was a serious student of Scripture and of the cultural and archaeological background of the biblical world. Her method was to begin with a solid base text (primarily the American Standard Version of 1901 for the New Testament) and then incorporate amplifications—explanatory phrases, alternative renderings, and synonyms—drawn from lexicons, word studies (such as Marvin Vincent’s), and other scholarly resources. These amplifications were set off by brackets, parentheses, and italics so that readers could distinguish the core translation from the expanded material.

In 1952 she received financial support from The Lockman Foundation of La Habra, California. Zondervan Publishing House joined the project as publisher. The Gospel of John appeared in 1954, followed by the complete Amplified New Testament in 1958. The New Testament was warmly received and received a notable endorsement from Billy Graham, which helped establish its early popularity.

## Completion of the Full Bible

With continuing support from Lockman and Zondervan, Siewert turned to the Old Testament. She produced two volumes: Job–Malachi in 1962 and Genesis–Esther in 1964. For the Old Testament she relied more heavily on the Revised Standard Version of 1952 as a base, while still consulting the original languages and related scholarship.

After Siewert’s work on the separate volumes, The Lockman Foundation engaged additional scholars to review and revise the entire text for consistency and accuracy. The first one-volume edition of *The Amplified Bible* was published in 1965. This edition established the characteristic look and method that would define the translation for decades.

## Later Revisions

**1987 Expanded Edition (later known as the Amplified Bible Classic Edition, or AMPC)**
An expanded revision added further amplifications, updated some language, and included additional footnotes. This edition remained widely available in both print and digital formats and is still valued by many longtime readers.

**2015 Update (Amplified Holy Bible / current AMP)**
Under the leadership of Dr. Robert G. Lambeth, president of The Lockman Foundation, a translation team thoroughly reviewed the text. The English was refreshed for contemporary readability, a substantial number of new amplifications were added (especially in the Old Testament), existing amplifications were refined or clarified, and additional footnotes and cross-references were supplied. The result is the edition most commonly available today.

## Translation Philosophy and Method

The Amplified Bible is fundamentally a formal-equivalence (essentially word-for-word) translation that has been expanded for clarity. Its distinctive feature is the systematic insertion of amplifications intended to convey nuances of the original languages that a single English word often cannot capture. Readers are thus given a range of possible shades of meaning within the text itself rather than only in footnotes or external study tools.

The Lockman Foundation’s fourfold aim—truthfulness to the original languages, grammatical correctness, understandability, and the proper exaltation of the Lord Jesus Christ—guided the work from the beginning and continues to shape later editions.

Textual bases include the American Standard Version tradition, critical editions of the Hebrew Masoretic Text, and Greek texts such as Westcott-Hort and Nestle, with consultation of lexicons, cognate languages, the Dead Sea Scrolls, the Septuagint, and other ancient versions.

## Reception and Use

The Amplified Bible has long been appreciated as a study aid. Its amplifications can open up richer understandings of key terms and help readers who lack access to original-language tools. At the same time, critics have noted that a word’s range of possible meanings does not mean every possible meaning is intended in every occurrence; the amplifications therefore require careful use. Some also find the bracketed material interrupts the flow of continuous reading.

Despite these caveats, the AMP has remained in continuous publication for nearly sixty years and continues to serve readers who desire both a readable English text and built-in lexical and contextual expansion.

## Place in the Larger Tradition

The Amplified Bible stands as the first major translation project of The Lockman Foundation—preceding even the New American Standard Bible—and as a creative extension of the American Standard Version tradition. By embedding interpretive and lexical expansion directly into the text, it occupies a unique niche among English Bibles: neither a pure formal translation nor a free paraphrase, but a hybrid designed to help ordinary readers grasp more of the richness of the original languages. Its successive editions reflect both fidelity to Siewert’s original vision and ongoing efforts to keep the language and amplifications clear for new generations of readers.
