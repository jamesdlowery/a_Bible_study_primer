# History of the Berean Standard Bible

**The Berean Standard Bible (BSB) is a modern English translation produced by the Bible Hub team and associated scholars, first appearing in full form around 2020.** Originally released as the Berean Study Bible, it was later standardized under the name Berean Standard Bible to distinguish it from study Bibles that feature extensive notes. The translation belongs to a coordinated family of “Berean” resources that include interlinear, literal, standard, and annotated tiers, all designed to keep the English text closely connected to the underlying Hebrew, Aramaic, and Greek.

What follows traces its origins, translation approach, publication history, distinctive features, and public-domain status.

## Origins and Vision

The name “Berean” is drawn from Acts 17:11, which describes the Jews of Berea as more noble-minded than those in Thessalonica because they “received the message with great eagerness and examined the Scriptures every day to see if these teachings were true.” The translators sought to produce a Bible that would encourage the same careful, Scripture-examining spirit.

The project grew out of the work of Bible Hub (an extensive online Bible study platform) and the Discovery Bible team. A relatively small translation committee of scholars associated with these ministries undertook the work, guided by an advisory group that included figures such as Dr. Grant Osborne (New Testament), Dr. Eugene H. Merrill (Old Testament), Dr. Gary Hill, and others. Funding and coordination came primarily from Bible Hub.

The goal was a completely new translation from the original languages that would be essentially literal yet smooth and readable in contemporary English, while remaining free for wide distribution and use.

## The Tiered Berean Approach

A distinctive feature of the Berean project is its multi-tier structure:

1. **Berean Interlinear Bible** – closely aligned to the original Greek and Hebrew words.
2. **Berean Literal Bible** – a more formal, word-for-word rendering that preserves core meanings and much of the original structure.
3. **Berean Standard Bible (BSB)** – a smooth, modern-English text suitable for reading, study, memorization, and public use, while still remaining traceable to the literal and interlinear tiers.
4. **Annotated / Emphasized versions** – further resources highlighting emphasis, nuance, and study helps.

The Standard Bible was developed by refining the interlinear and literal work through multiple rounds of editing (reportedly seven full passes) to achieve literary quality without sacrificing accuracy or the ability to link back to the original-language roots.

## Publication Timeline

- **2016**: The New Testament of the Berean Study Bible appeared in print and online.
- **2020**: The complete Bible (Old and New Testaments) was published.
- **2021–2022**: The title was standardized to “Berean Standard Bible (BSB)” in newer printings and software listings to avoid confusion with traditional study Bibles that contain extensive commentary.
- **30 April 2023**: The text was formally dedicated to the public domain (CC0), making it free for any use, modification, or distribution, with attribution appreciated but not required.
- Subsequent printings and digital updates have continued, with the text remaining freely available on Bible Hub, dedicated websites, and various open platforms.

## Textual Basis and Translation Philosophy

For the Old Testament the translators relied primarily on the Masoretic Text (as represented in standard critical editions such as *Biblia Hebraica Stuttgartensia*), with attention to the Dead Sea Scrolls and other ancient witnesses. For the New Testament they drew upon modern critical editions, including Nestle-Aland, the SBL Greek New Testament, and related sources, while also consulting a range of manuscript traditions.

The philosophy is essentially literal (formal equivalence with attention to readability). The translators aimed for a text that is accurate to the original wording and meaning, natural in modern English, and transparent enough that readers can readily connect English renderings back to Greek or Hebrew roots via the accompanying interlinear and literal tiers. The result occupies a middle ground on the translation spectrum—more literal than highly dynamic versions, yet smoother than the most rigidly formal ones.

## Distinctive Features and Availability

Because the BSB was designed from the outset for digital as well as print use, and because it was placed in the public domain, it has become one of the more freely shareable modern English translations. It is offered without cost for reading, downloading, software integration, and further development. The translators have emphasized that the work ascribes to a high view of Scripture’s inerrancy and to historic evangelical orthodoxy.

The relatively small size of the translation team has been noted as contributing to internal consistency, while community feedback from the large user base of Bible Hub helped refine the text over successive iterations.

## Place in the Larger Tradition

The Berean Standard Bible represents a twenty-first-century effort to produce a free, accurate, and readable English Bible that remains closely tethered to the original languages. By pairing a smooth standard text with interlinear and literal companions, and by releasing the work into the public domain, its makers have sought to serve both individual students of Scripture and the wider church in the spirit of the Bereans of Acts 17. It stands as a distinctive contribution among recent formal-to-mediating translations, notable less for institutional sponsorship than for its digital-native origins, tiered design, and open availability.
