# History of the World English Bible

**The World English Bible (WEB) is a modern English translation of the Scriptures that is entirely in the public domain.** It originated in the mid-1990s as an update of the American Standard Version of 1901, undertaken so that a high-quality, contemporary English Bible could be freely copied, posted online, and distributed without copyright restrictions or royalty payments. The work was led by Michael Paul Johnson with the assistance of numerous volunteer editors and proofreaders under the auspices of Rainbow Missions, Inc. A stable complete text was reached around 2020.

What follows traces its origins, method, distinctive features, and place among free English Bibles.

## Origins and Vision

In March 1994, while driving and praying, Michael Paul Johnson became convinced that there was a need for a modern English Bible translation that could be freely shared in electronic and print form without legal barriers. Existing modern versions were under copyright, limiting their unrestricted distribution on the internet and in other media. Johnson began studying Greek and Hebrew and produced initial drafts of the Gospel and letters of John, which he shared on Usenet and mailing lists for feedback.

Realizing that a completely independent translation from the original languages would take far longer than a single lifetime, he decided to base the project on the American Standard Version of 1901. The ASV was highly regarded for its accuracy and formal style, and—crucially—it had entered the public domain. The goal became to modernize the ASV’s language while retaining its essential fidelity and, where appropriate, adjusting the New Testament toward the Byzantine Majority Text.

The name “World English Bible” was chosen to reflect its intended availability to English speakers everywhere and its primary means of distribution via the World Wide Web.

## Translation Process

The revision process involved multiple stages. An initial computer-assisted pass updated roughly a thousand archaic words and forms. Subsequent manual passes by volunteers added modern punctuation (the ASV lacked quotation marks), smoothed grammar and word order into contemporary English, and checked the text against the original languages, especially where textual variants or unclear meanings occurred. Each book typically underwent about seven rounds of editing and proofreading.

For the Old Testament the primary base remained the Masoretic Text (as represented in *Biblia Hebraica Stuttgartensia*), with attention to the Dead Sea Scrolls and other evidence. For the New Testament the translators moved from the critical text underlying the ASV toward the Majority Text (Robinson-Pierpont) in places where the difference was significant. The translation philosophy is predominantly formal equivalence (word-for-word), aiming for accuracy and clarity in modern English.

A distinctive feature in the classic WEB is the rendering of the Tetragrammaton as “Yahweh” rather than the traditional “LORD.” (British and some other editions of the WEB family use “LORD” or “GOD” in small capitals.)

## Publication and Variants

Drafts began appearing online in the mid-1990s, with an early complete draft in 1997. The text continued to be refined for many years. By 2020 the main edition was regarded as stable. Because the work is in the public domain, it has been widely mirrored, printed by various parties, and incorporated into software and websites.

Several related editions exist within the broader WEB family: - The standard American-English WEB (sometimes using “Yahweh”) - British/International spelling editions (WEBBE) - Messianic editions that employ traditional Hebrew names and terms (World Messianic Bible / WMB and variants) - Editions that include the Deuterocanonical/Apocryphal books

The name “World English Bible” is retained as a trademark for faithful copies; substantially altered versions are asked not to use the name, in order to avoid confusion.

## Distinctive Character

The WEB’s primary distinction is legal rather than stylistic: it is a complete, modern, essentially formal-equivalence English Bible that anyone may copy, print, distribute, or adapt without payment or permission. This openness has made it especially useful for websites, apps, open-source projects, mission work, and individuals who wish to share Scripture freely. Its language is clearer and more contemporary than the 1901 ASV while remaining recognizably in the same formal tradition.

Because it began as a volunteer, internet-based project rather than a commercial or denominational undertaking, it has a decentralized, collaborative character. Quality control rested on repeated public review and the editorial oversight of Johnson and the volunteer team.

## Place in the Larger Tradition

The World English Bible stands in the lineage of the American Standard Version and, through it, the Revised Version and the King James tradition. At the same time, it represents a distinctly late-twentieth- and early-twenty-first-century phenomenon: a translation conceived for the digital age and deliberately released from copyright so that the text itself can travel as freely as the gospel it proclaims. In an era when most major English Bibles remain under copyright, the WEB continues to fill the role its originator envisioned—a reliable, modern English Bible that belongs to everyone.
