# Leviticus: Significant Textual Variants Across Ten Translations

**Translations covered:** AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, RSV2CE

Leviticus is one of the textually calmest books in the Old Testament, and the research pass behind this project did not find a case where the ten tracked translations actually render a verse with different wording that changes its meaning. The items below are real variants in the wider manuscript tradition, but none of them produces a split among the ten tracked translations.

## Side notes: cases where all ten translations agree, but a real variant exists elsewhere in the manuscript tradition

- **Leviticus 17:3–4** — the Dead Sea Scrolls and Septuagint preserve a longer text specifying additional detail about slaughtering animals outside the camp; MT is shorter at this point. All ten tracked translations follow the shorter MT reading, and none flags the longer version in a footnote.
- **Leviticus 18:21 / 20:2–5** — these verses concern child sacrifice "to Molech." A small number of Septuagint manuscripts show a scribal-level confusion between "Molech" (a god's name) and a similar-looking Hebrew word meaning something closer to "ruler" or "king," but this is generally regarded as an isolated Greek scribal error rather than a genuine competing Hebrew reading. All ten tracked translations render "Molech."
