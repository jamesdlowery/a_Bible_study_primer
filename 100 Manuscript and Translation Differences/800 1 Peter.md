# 1 Peter: Significant Textual Variants Across Ten Translations

**Translations covered:** AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, RSV2CE

---

## 1. 1 Peter 3:15 — "sanctify the Lord God," or "sanctify Christ as Lord"?

**The variant:** Instructing believers on how to face hostile questioning about their faith, the majority of later Greek manuscripts (and so the Textus Receptus) read "sanctify the Lord God in your hearts" (*Kyrion ton Theon*) — closely echoing the Old Testament language of Isaiah 8:13, "sanctify the LORD of hosts himself." The earliest and generally most highly regarded manuscripts instead read "sanctify Christ as Lord" (*Kyrion ton Christon*) — a one-word substitution (Christon for Theon) that directly applies the exact language Isaiah uses for God himself to Christ specifically.

**Why it matters:** Both readings affirm Christ's full deity elsewhere in the letter and in the New Testament generally, so no core doctrine is at stake either way — but the "Christ as Lord" reading, if original, is itself a notable, explicit instance of New Testament language directly transferring an Old Testament description of God onto Jesus, at exactly the point where Peter is alluding to Isaiah's own "sanctify the LORD" language.

**Translations — a real split:**
- **"Sanctify the Lord God":** KJV ("sanctify the Lord God in your hearts"), NKJV (same).
- **"Sanctify Christ as Lord" / "regard Christ the Lord as holy":** ESV, NIV, CSB, NASB, LSB, NLT all render some form of "Christ as Lord" rather than "the Lord God," following the earlier manuscripts.
- I don't have solid, separately verified data for AMP and RSV2CE on this specific verse.
