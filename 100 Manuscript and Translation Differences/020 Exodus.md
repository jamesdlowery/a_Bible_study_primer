# Exodus: Significant Textual Variants Across Ten Translations

**Translations covered:** AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, RSV2CE

The research pass behind this project did not confirm a case in Exodus where the ten tracked translations actually render a verse with different wording that changes its meaning. That may reflect Exodus genuinely having fewer such cases relative to books like Genesis or Deuteronomy, or it may reflect a gap in how thoroughly this book was checked -- worth stating plainly rather than implying a completeness that has not been verified.

---

## Side note: a case where all ten translations agree, but a real variant exists elsewhere in the manuscript tradition

- **Exodus 1:5** — MT gives the number of Jacob's household who went down to Egypt as "seventy"; the Septuagint and a Dead Sea Scrolls fragment instead give "seventy-five" -- the figure Stephen uses when he quotes this same tradition in Acts 7:14. (Deuteronomy 10:22's parallel statement of the same fact is textually stable at "seventy" in both MT and the Septuagint, so this specific variant is confined to Exodus 1:5 itself, not the wider tradition about Jacob's household.) All ten tracked translations follow MT's "seventy" at Exodus 1:5, and all ten likewise render Acts 7:14's "seventy-five" as the New Testament text itself states it, without harmonizing the two numbers against each other.
- **Exodus 12:40** — MT states the Israelites lived "in Egypt" 430 years; the Septuagint and Samaritan Pentateuch both add "and in Canaan," splitting the 430 years between the two locations (relevant to a longstanding chronological question about how long the Egyptian sojourn actually lasted, compared with Genesis' genealogical data). All ten tracked translations follow MT's "in Egypt" alone, without the addition.
