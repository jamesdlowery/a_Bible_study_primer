# Job: Significant Textual Variants Across Ten Translations

**Translations covered:** AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, RSV2CE

---

## Job 13:15 — "though he slay me, yet will I trust him," or "I have no hope"?

**The variant:** This is a Kethiv/Qere case. As written (Kethiv), the Hebrew reads *lo* spelled with an aleph (לא), "not" — giving "though he slay me, I have no hope." The traditional marginal correction (Qere) has *lo* spelled with a vav (לו), "in him" — giving "though he slay me, yet will I trust in him." The two words are pronounced identically and differ by one easily-confused letter.

**How the ten translations render it:**
- **"I will hope/trust in him" (following the Qere), in the main text:** KJV ("yet will I trust in him"), NKJV ("yet I will trust Him"), NASB ("I will hope in Him"), AMP ("yet will I trust Him"), and also **ESV** ("I will hope in him," with a footnote noting the alternative "I have no hope") and **NIV** and **CSB** ("yet I will hope in him" / "I will still hope in him," each with a footnote giving "I have no hope" as the Hebrew's literal alternative).
- **A reading closer to "I have no hope" (following the Kethiv):** the RSV tradition (RSV, NRSV) renders this as "I have no hope," and **RSV2CE** most likely follows that same family reading, though its specific printing is worth checking directly.
- **NLT** takes a third path rather than a clean rendering of either Kethiv or Qere: "God might kill me, but I have no other hope" — a paraphrase that leans toward the despairing sense but is its own construction, not simply "I have no hope."
- I don't have solid, separately verified data for LSB on this specific verse.

**The difference in meaning:** One reading is among the most quoted expressions of faith-under-suffering in the Bible; the other has Job voicing raw despair while still defiantly planning to argue his case, but explicitly without hope.
