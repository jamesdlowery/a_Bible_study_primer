# Daniel: Significant Textual Variants Across Ten Translations

**Translations covered:** AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, RSV2CE

As with Esther elsewhere in this collection, Daniel's most significant textual difference isn't a wording variant within a shared verse — it's the presence or absence of entire additional passages, tracking Catholic vs. Protestant canon rather than manuscript preference.

---

## The Additions to Daniel — entire passages present in one of the ten tracked translations, absent from the other nine

**The variant:** The Greek tradition of Daniel includes three passages with no counterpart in the Hebrew/Aramaic text: the Prayer of Azariah and Song of the Three Young Men (expanding the fiery-furnace episode in chapter 3), and two additional stories, Susanna and Bel and the Dragon (typically numbered as chapters 13 and 14). The Council of Trent affirmed this material as canonical for Roman Catholics.

**How the ten translations handle it:**
- **RSV2CE** includes the Additions as canonical text, integrated at their traditional points (Susanna and Bel and the Dragon as chapters 13–14, the Prayer of Azariah and Song of the Three within chapter 3).
- **AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, and NLT** — the other nine — do not include this material.

**The difference in meaning:** As with Esther, this is a difference in how much material is in the book at all, not a wording difference within a shared passage. Comparing RSV2CE's Daniel against any of the other nine means comparing a longer book (with two additional short stories and an extended prayer) against a shorter one, not two translations of the same text.
