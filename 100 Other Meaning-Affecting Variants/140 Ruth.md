# Ruth: Significant Textual Variants Across Ten Translations

**Translations covered:** AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, RSV2CE

Ruth, like Leviticus, is textually one of the calmest books in the Old Testament. The research pass behind this project did not find a case where the ten tracked translations render a verse with different wording that changes its meaning.

---

## Side notes: cases in the wider tradition that don't split the ten tracked translations

- **Ruth 3:15** — a famous Kethiv/Qere-adjacent case: the earliest printed 1611 KJV read "he went into the city" (referring to Boaz), while the standard 1769 KJV revision (the text basis for virtually all KJVs printed today, and for NKJV) corrected this to "she went into the city" (referring to Ruth), matching the Hebrew Qere. All ten of your current translations read "she," so there's no live split among them today — this is really a case of an older printing history being resolved rather than an ongoing manuscript variant.
- **Ruth 4:5** — the Hebrew as written (Kethiv) could be read as "I have acquired," but the traditional marginal correction (Qere) reads "you must also acquire" (referring to Boaz addressing the nearer kinsman-redeemer). All ten of your translations follow the Qere, "you [the kinsman] must also acquire/buy."
