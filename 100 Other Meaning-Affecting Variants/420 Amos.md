# Amos: Significant Textual Variants Across Ten Translations

**Translations covered:** AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, RSV2CE

A systematic search did not find a case in Amos where the ten tracked translations render a verse with different, meaning-changing wording. The one well-known variant in this book, discussed below, is a case where all ten translations agree with each other.

---

## Side note: a case where all ten translations agree, but the variant has real New Testament significance

- **Amos 9:11–12** — MT reads that Israel's restored kingdom will "possess the remnant of **Edom**." The Septuagint instead reads "the remnant of **mankind** ... may seek [the Lord]" — a real difference in the underlying Hebrew consonants. This matters because in Acts 15, James quotes the passage in its Septuagint form as scriptural support for admitting Gentiles into the church without circumcision. All ten of your translations render Amos 9:12 itself following MT, "the remnant of Edom" — several (ESV, NIV, NASB) footnote the Septuagint reading and its use in Acts 15:17, but none adopts it into Amos's own main text.
