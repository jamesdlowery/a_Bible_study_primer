# Jeremiah: Significant Textual Variants Across Ten Translations

**Translations covered:** AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, RSV2CE

Jeremiah's most famous textual feature is a book-wide structural one, not a single-verse wording split, and all ten tracked translations agree on which edition to follow. A systematic search did not find a separate verse within Jeremiah where the ten tracked translations render the same words with meaning-changing differences.

---

## Side note: a whole-book structural difference, not reflected among the ten tracked translations

- **Jeremiah, throughout** — the Septuagint's version of Jeremiah is roughly one-eighth shorter than the Masoretic Text and arranges some material (notably the oracles against foreign nations) in a substantially different order. A Dead Sea Scrolls Hebrew fragment (4QJerᵇ) confirms the shorter, reordered edition existed as a genuine independent Hebrew text, not just a loose Greek abridgment — making Jeremiah, alongside Samuel, one of the two clearest examples of two authentic literary editions of a Hebrew Bible book circulating in antiquity. All ten of your translations follow the longer Masoretic edition, in Masoretic order.
