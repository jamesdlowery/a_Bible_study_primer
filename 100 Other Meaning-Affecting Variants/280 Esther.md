# Esther: Significant Textual Variants Across Ten Translations

**Translations covered:** AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, NLT, RSV2CE

This isn't a single-verse wording variant, but it's the largest and most consequential textual difference affecting Esther among the ten tracked translations, so it's called out here rather than as a side note.

---

## The Additions to Esther — an entire block of material present in one of the ten tracked translations, absent from the other nine

**The variant:** The Hebrew Masoretic Text of Esther never mentions God, prayer, or explicit religious practice at all. A Greek edition of Esther that began circulating in the last two centuries BC adds six substantial passages — roughly 107 additional verses, nearly the length of the Hebrew book itself — including Mordecai's prophetic dream, the full text of the royal edicts, and explicit prayers by Mordecai and Esther invoking God by name. The Council of Trent (1546) affirmed the whole book, Hebrew text plus Greek additions, as canonical for Roman Catholics.

**How the ten translations handle it:**
- **RSV2CE** includes the Additions, integrated into the narrative at their traditional insertion points, as part of its canonical text of Esther.
- **AMP, CSB, ESV, KJV, NKJV, NASB, LSB, NIV, and NLT** — all nine of the others — contain only the Hebrew-based text and do not include this material anywhere in their standard editions.

**The difference in meaning:** This isn't a wording difference within a shared verse — it's the presence or absence of entire passages. The Hebrew-only Esther is a story where God's involvement is implied only through timing and circumstance; the version with the Additions is a book with explicit prayers and direct statements that God is acting. If you compare RSV2CE's Esther against any of the other nine side by side, you're comparing two different lengths of the same core story, not two translations of identical material.
